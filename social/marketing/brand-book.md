# MENVA brand book

A practical messaging and voice guide. Anyone writing for MENVA — a freelancer, an intern, an agency, Abdullah — should be able to open this file and write on-brand copy without asking questions.

Built strictly from `BRIEF.md`, `design/MENVA.DESIGN.md`, `social/content/plan.md`, and the research in `social/marketing/research/`. No stats, prices, names or claims appear here beyond what those files establish as true. Where a source is external research (not a MENVA fact), it is labelled as such — see Section 8.

---

## 1. Brand essence

**Purpose.** Lahore diners order blind — a menu photo, a PDF, or just a name on a list — and hope the plate that arrives matches what they imagined. MENVA closes that gap: it shows the real dish, at true size, on the diner's own table, before they order.

**Promise.** *See it before you order it.*

**Personality.** Calm, warm, a little playful, never cartoonish — exactly as `BRIEF.md` and `MENVA.DESIGN.md` set it. In practice:
- **Calm**: no hype, no urgency language, no exclamation-mark stacking.
- **Warm**: talks like a friend who's been to the restaurant already, not a corporate account.
- **A little playful**: dry humour, one relatable observation, one Roman Urdu line that lands like something a person would actually say.
- **Never cartoonish**: no mascots as the default voice, no gimmicks, no forced jokes. (Cheffy, the small mascot in the product loader, is opt-in per restaurant and off by default for fine dining — it is a UI detail, not the brand's voice.)

**What MENVA is not.**
- Not an app — it opens in the phone's browser. Never call it "our app" or "download."
- Not an ordering or payment platform. There is no cart, no checkout. The waiter stays in charge.
- Not an AI image generator. Every dish shown is a real 3D scan of the real plate — never a render, never AI-generated food.
- Not a stock-photo or influencer-styled food brand. No borrowed or generic "foodie content" imagery.
- Not a loud, hype-driven startup voice. No "revolutionary," no "game-changer," no countdown urgency.
- Not (yet) a multi-restaurant directory, a delivery app, or a reviews platform. It is a menu tool for restaurants that use it.
- Not able to promise sales results. No AOV or "orders increased" claims about MENVA itself exist yet — see Section 8.

## 2. Messaging house

### Umbrella message

**See it before you order it.**

Everything else — every pillar, every audience message — supports this one idea: MENVA turns a menu decision from a guess into something you can actually see, at real size, on your own table.

### The pillars

Each pillar's proof points are facts drawn directly from `BRIEF.md`. Nothing here is a number, a promise of results, or an inclusion of the pilot price — those aren't established facts.

| Pillar | What it means | Proof points (true product facts only) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Real, not rendered** | Every dish is the actual plate, not an idea of it. | A real 3D scan of the plate, not a photo or illustration · no AI-generated food, ever · no stock photos |
| **2. True to size, on your table** | The dish isn't just 3D — it's placed in AR at the size it actually is. | Places the dish in AR on the diner's own table at true size · locked scale — it can't be pinch-resized in AR, on purpose · phones without AR support can still turn the dish in 3D |
| **3. No app, no friction** | Nothing to install, nothing to learn. | Opens straight in the phone's browser after a QR scan · works in Safari on iPhone and Chrome on Android · no login, no download |
| **4. Built for how the restaurant actually runs** | Designed around real Lahore restaurant conditions and real service, not a demo environment. | The dish photo shows first, so it works on slow restaurant wifi — the 3D follows · "show the waiter": diners add dishes and notes (like "no onions") to a list and turn the phone to the waiter · no online ordering or payment — the waiter stays in charge · restaurants get pilot analytics: scans, dish opens, AR launches, lists shown to waiters |

Use 2–3 pillars per piece of content, not all four — a single post or pitch line should feel like one idea, not a feature list.

### Messaging by audience

Same umbrella message, different entry point. Don't mix these in one piece of copy — a diner-facing Instagram caption and a restaurant-facing pitch line are different documents, even when they say something similar.

| Audience | Lead with | Sounds like | Never say |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diners** | Confidence and relief — no more guessing, no more "that's not what I pictured." | "See it before you order it." / "Order se pehle dekh lo." Short, warm, one Roman Urdu line, ends with something to send, save, tag or comment. | The pilot price, the pilot restaurant's name, "AI," any sales stat. |
| **Restaurant owners** | Plating gets the credit it deserves; the waiter stays fully in charge; there's a way to see interest before a dish is ordered. | Direct, concrete, no hype: what the diner sees, what the restaurant gets to see (pilot analytics), how the pilot works. Pilot price PKR 25,000 (Lahore) can be stated — but **never say what it includes**, since that isn't established. | Any inclusion of the pilot price, any promise of an order-value or sales lift, any competitor's stat presented as typical. |
| **Chefs** | Respect for the actual work — the plate is shown exactly as it's plated, not reinterpreted. | "This is the plate you made, scanned as it is — not a stylised photo, not an AI reimagining of it." | Anything implying the scan "improves" or "beautifies" the dish — the pitch is accuracy, not enhancement. |
| **Press** | What MENVA actually is, plainly, with the founder named and no inflation. | "A QR-to-AR dish menu for restaurants in Lahore. A diner scans the QR, taps a dish, and sees a real 3D scan of the plate — then places it on their own table in AR at true size. No app. Built by Abdullah." | Any stat not sourced with a URL, any client name beyond what's cleared, any comparison to a competitor's number as if it were MENVA's own result. |
| **Investors** | Category and mechanics, honestly framed: a small, fragmented, largely US/UK category with no dominant winner and no AR-menu competitor currently in Pakistan (`research/competitors.md`, Section 1–2) — MENVA's real-scans-only and locked true-size rules are genuine differentiators against vendors that lean on AI-generated dishes or resizable renders. | Plain, evidence-labelled: what's confirmed (product mechanics, one pilot), what's early (four dish scans, one restaurant), what's borrowed evidence (the Oxford/JAMS study — see Section 8) vs. what's a MENVA result (nothing yet). | Presenting any competitor's or academic study's numbers as MENVA's own traction. Presenting the four-scan pilot inventory as a finished catalogue. |

## 4. Voice rules

- **Sentence case everywhere** — headlines, buttons, captions, slide text. Capitalise only proper nouns (MENVA, Lahore, Safari, Chrome) and the start of a sentence.
- **No emoji.** Not in captions, not in buttons, not in DMs sent as MENVA, not in error states. This is a hard brand rule, not a style preference.
- **CTA verbs stay concrete and calm**: scan, tap, see, place, turn, add, show, send, save, tag, comment, DM. Avoid "act now," "don't miss out," "click here," "shop now" (there's nothing to shop).
- **Error and apology tone**: explain what happened and what to do next. No blanket "Something went wrong," no over-apologising ("we're SO sorry!!"), no blaming the user. One calm line, then a next step. (This mirrors `MENVA.DESIGN.md`'s own guardrail: "Error copy explains what happened and what to do — no apologies, no vague 'Something went wrong'.")
- **Humour limits**: dry and observational, about dining culture (the over-orderer, the waiter interruption, the group-chat "you choose"), never about the food's quality, a competitor by name, cricket losses, politics, or religion. One joke per post, not stacked. If a joke needs an emoji or three exclamation marks to land, cut it.
- **One idea per post/line.** Don't combine a product fact, a joke, and a CTA into one sentence — let each carry its own line.

### Do and don't (14 pairs)

| Do | Don't | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "See it before you order it." | "Experience next-level dining with revolutionary AR technology!" | No hype words, no exclamation stacking. |
| "The photo loads first, always." | "Blazing-fast performance, guaranteed." | State the real mechanism, not a vague speed claim. |
| "A real 3D scan of the actual plate." | "AI-powered hyper-realistic visualization." | MENVA uses real scans, never AI-generated imagery — never imply otherwise. |
| "True size. It can't be resized in AR." | "An immersive augmented reality experience." | Say the specific, true feature, not a generic AR buzz-phrase. |
| "Add it to your list, then show the waiter." | "Order now." | MENVA has no ordering. Never use ordering language. |
| "Works in Safari and Chrome. No app." | "Download our app today." | There is no app. |
| "Scan, then decide." | "Scan NOW or miss out!!" | Calm, not urgent — nothing is actually scarce here. |
| "We couldn't load the model — here it is in 360° instead." | "Oops! Something went wrong." | Say what happened and what the person gets instead. |
| "Please confirm with your server." | Guessing an allergen, ingredient, or halal status. | Never fabricate data that isn't confirmed. |
| "An Oxford field study found AR menus roughly doubled dessert spend, in one UK restaurant." | "AR menus double your sales." | Cite external research as external — never as a MENVA result. See Section 8. |
| "Pehle dekho, phir order." | "OMG this is so lit fr fr 🔥🔥🔥" | On-brand Roman Urdu line vs. imported slang plus banned emoji. |
| "Send this to the friend who takes 20 minutes to order." | "Like, comment, and subscribe for more content!" | Platform-native CTA (Instagram DM culture), not a generic social-media line. |
| "Bas kar yaar, itna bada?!" (one natural term, in context) | "Yaar this is like, so extra, no cap, it's giving main character energy." | One local slang term used naturally beats several stacked, imported ones — see Section 5. |
| "The waiter stays in charge." | "Skip the wait — order instantly." | False: MENVA doesn't take orders. Never suggest a feature that doesn't exist. |

## 5. Roman Urdu style guide

### Spelling conventions

Pick one spelling and use it every time — consistency matters more than any single "correct" spelling, since Roman Urdu has no single standard.

| Word | Use this spelling | Not this |
|---|---|---|
| hai | hai | hy, hae |
| hain | hain | hain't, hein |
| kya | kya | kia, kiya |
| yaar | yaar | yar |
| pehle | pehle | pehlay, pahle |
| dekho | dekho | dekhoo |
| dekh lo | dekh lo (two words) | dekhlo (joined) |
| khana | khana | khaana |
| mangwao | mangwao | mangwaao |
| karo / karna | karo / karna | karro, karnaa |
| nahi | nahi | nai, nahin |
| phir | phir | fir |
| asli | asli | assli |
| bas | bas | bass |
| aana | aana | ana |
| apna / apni / apki | apna / apni / apki | apnaa, apkey |
| order, waiter, scan, table, AR, app | keep as English loanwords, no Roman-Urdu respelling | "wetar," "skain," etc. |

If a new word comes up that isn't in this table, pick the simplest, most phonetic spelling and add it here so future copy stays consistent (flag it — see Section 10).

### When to use Roman Urdu vs. English vs. Urdu script

- **English leads.** Every piece of public copy reads clearly for someone who only reads English — this is the brief's own rule ("English first, plus a natural Lahori Roman Urdu line").
- **Roman Urdu is one supporting line**, not a full translation. It should sound like something a Lahori friend would actually say about the same idea, not a literal word-for-word conversion of the English line. `plan.md` states this directly: "Not translated word for word: said the way a Lahori would say it."
- **Full Urdu script is not used in MENVA brand copy.** The brief specifies English + Roman Urdu only; there's no product or research basis here for Urdu script. If a use case comes up (e.g. a restaurant partner wants Urdu-script signage), that's outside this book — flag it for Abdullah rather than improvising a convention.

### Code-switching: natural vs. cringe

From `research/genz-culture.md`'s slang research, applied as brand rules:

**Natural**
- One clean English hook, then one short, natural Roman Urdu line — the pattern used throughout `calendar.json` and recommended by the research.
- At most **one** slang/idiom term per caption (yaar, bas kar, full on, dawat, scene on hai), and only where a real person would actually say it in that sentence.
- Real, specific local references when they're true to the content (an actual Lahore area, a real dining moment) — specificity reads as authentic.
- Product terms stay untranslated and un-sloganised: "show the waiter" is always exactly that, in English, never turned into slang.

**Cringe (avoid)**
- Stacking three or four slang terms in one caption — reads as a brand trying too hard.
- Imported global slang not verified as natural in Lahori food talk (e.g. "OP," "lit," "no cap," "it's giving...") — these test as risky or unverified in the research and should be left out of brand copy.
- Forcing a Roman Urdu line onto an English sentence that already says the same thing word for word — it should add a local voice, not just repeat the English.
- Riding a fast-moving meme (cricket loss, drama-serial moment) without a same-day, very light, non-partisan angle — slow or forced timing reads worse than skipping it.

## 8. Claims policy

**The rule, from `BRIEF.md`:** never invent stats, prices, client names, testimonials, dish data or results. Any number cited needs a source URL. Label estimates as estimates. If it can't be verified, say so.

### What MENVA can claim (public-facing, no source needed — these are confirmed product facts)

- It's a QR-to-AR dish menu for restaurants in Lahore, Pakistan.
- The menu opens in the phone's browser after a QR scan — no app, works in Safari on iPhone and Chrome on Android.
- Diners see a real 3D scan of the plate, then can place it on their own table in AR at true size; it can't be pinch-resized in AR.
- Phones without AR support can still turn the dish in 3D.
- Built for slow restaurant wifi: the dish photo shows first, the 3D follows.
- "Show the waiter": diners add dishes and notes to a list and turn the phone to the waiter. There's no online ordering or payment.
- Restaurants get pilot analytics: scans, dish opens, AR launches, lists shown to waiters.
- The pilot price is PKR 25,000 (Lahore) — restaurant-facing only, never diner-facing, and **never state what it includes**, since that isn't established.
- No AI-generated food imagery is ever used — only real scans.

### What MENVA cannot claim

- Any sales, order-value, or conversion result for MENVA itself. None exists yet — there is one pilot, and it has not been measured.
- The pilot restaurant's name, or any detail that would identify it, without the founder's sign-off.
- Either unconfirmed dish name ("the green plate," "the trio" as descriptions only — the actual names behind them are not confirmed).
- What the PKR 25,000 pilot price includes.
- Any competitor's or vendor's stat (e.g. Kabaq/QReal's self-reported "20–26% AOV uplift") presented as typical, verified, or applicable to MENVA. Per `research/competitors.md`, that figure is an unaudited vendor claim with no visible methodology — it may be mentioned only as "one vendor's own, unverified claim," never repeated as fact.
- "Halal-certified" as a marketing hook. The product shows a halal mark (yes/no/unconfirmed) as a trust feature — per `research/genz-culture.md`, Gen Z awareness of formal halal certification specifically is low, and certification is not something MENVA has obtained or claims.

### Citing outside research correctly

Two pieces of outside research come up often enough to need a standard citation pattern. Both must always be labelled as someone else's finding, in someone else's market, never as evidence about MENVA:

1. **The Oxford/JAMS AR-menu study** (Fritz, Hadi & Stephen, *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, 2023 — see `research/competitors.md` Section 3). Findings: in a field study of 101 diners at one UK restaurant, an AR dessert menu lifted dessert order rate from 18% to 41.2% and dessert spend from $1.38 to $2.93 on average.
   - **Correct**: "An Oxford field study found an AR dessert menu roughly doubled dessert spend, in one UK restaurant — not a MENVA result."
   - **Incorrect**: "AR menus double your dessert sales" / "Studies show a 112% increase" (stated with no source, or implied to be about MENVA).
   - Any use of this study in outward-facing material (a deck, a press pitch, a sales page) needs Abdullah's sign-off first — see `research/competitors.md`'s own "Needs Abdullah" note.
2. **Vendor and market-size figures in `research/competitors.md`** (e.g. Kabaq/QReal's claims, market-size estimates from templated report sites). These are explicitly flagged low-to-medium confidence in that document. Never state them as fact; if used at all, they must carry both their source and a confidence label ("a vendor's own unaudited claim," "an estimate from a market-report site, not independently verified").

**General rule:** if a number, name, or result isn't in `BRIEF.md`, this book, or cited with its own source URL and confidence label, don't write it.

## 9. Ten-point checklist before publishing anything

1. Does it lead with "see it before you order it" (or a message that clearly supports it), not a generic marketing line?
2. Is it sentence case, with zero emoji?
3. Is every claim in Section 8's "can claim" list — and nothing from the "cannot claim" list?
4. Is the pilot restaurant unnamed, its logo absent from every image, and the two unconfirmed dish names left unnamed ("the green plate," "the trio")?
5. Is every dish shown a real scan — no AI-generated food, no stock photography?
6. Is the PKR 25,000 pilot price used only in restaurant-facing content, with no stated inclusions, and never in diner-facing content?
7. Is any outside statistic (the Oxford study, a vendor claim, a market-size figure) labelled with its source and never presented as a MENVA result?
8. Does it use only approved or clearly-marked-candidate lines from Section 3, and does the Roman Urdu line sound like something a Lahori would actually say — not a literal translation?
9. Is there exactly one call to action (send, save, tag, comment, DM "PILOT," etc.), and no ordering language ("order now," "buy," "checkout")?
10. Does the visual follow one accent colour, MENVA's palette and type (or the correct restaurant theme, never mixed), with no second accent on the same screen?
