This is a ready-to-use, paste-and-go sales kit for selling the MENVA AR menu pilot to Lahore restaurants. It follows `BRIEF.md`: no invented stats, no client names beyond "[pilot restaurant]", no promised pilot inclusions, no emoji. Public-facing scripts are marked; everything else is an internal working document for Abdullah.

## 1. Ideal customer profile (ICP)

### Who to target first, and why

MENVA's AR effect needs three things to land: a dish worth looking at twice, a diner who already photographs their food, and a restaurant that can absorb a PKR 25,000 pilot without a committee decision. That points to five categories, in priority order:

| Priority | Category | Why they convert faster |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Steakhouses / premium casual grill** | Big, visually dramatic plates (steak boards, sizzling platters). Diners already ask "how big is the cut" — AR answers that directly. Higher average bill, so PKR 25,000 reads as small money to the owner. |
| 2 | **Fine dining** | Menu is already a status symbol; a 3D/AR menu fits the brand instead of feeling like a gimmick. Owners are used to spending on experience (lighting, plating, glassware) and will recognize AR as the same category of spend. |
| 3 | **Cafés with photogenic desserts / all-day brunch spots** | Desserts and brunch plates are already what guests photograph and post (our observation, not a measured figure), so AR is a natural extension of what they do on Instagram anyway. |
| 4 | **New openings (pre-launch or first 90 days)** | No legacy menu, no internal resistance ("we've always done it this way"), and they're actively looking for a launch story/press angle. Easiest "yes" if you can catch them before their menu is printed. |
| 5 | **Restaurants with a large Instagram following but a text-only or PDF menu** | Proof the demand for visual content exists — the follower count is the evidence, the flat menu is the gap. Point out the specific mismatch when you pitch. |

### Screening criteria (use this before you spend time on a lead)

A restaurant is a **good first call** if it has at least 3 of these 5:
1. Table service (not counter/fast food) — AR needs a table to place the dish on.
2. At least one visually strong "hero" dish (steak, platter, layered dessert, shareable centerpiece).
3. Decent wifi or 4G coverage in the dining room (ask, don't assume — walk in and check your own phone signal).
4. An active Instagram or TikTok account, ideally one that already posts food photos/video.
5. A single, reachable decision maker (owner-operator or GM with real authority) — avoid chains where the branch manager can't approve PKR 25,000 without head office.

**Disqualify or deprioritize:** delivery-only/cloud kitchens (no physical table to demo AR on), very small dhaba-style setups (no wifi, no photography culture, price sensitivity too high for a paid pilot), and large multi-branch chains in your first 90 days (slow approval chains — come back to them once you have a case study).

### Areas to work, in order

1. **MM Alam Road / Gulberg** — highest density of premium casual and fine dining in one walkable strip; also where competitors and press already look, so a MENVA sighting here travels fastest by word of mouth.
2. **DHA (Phase 4, 5, 6, 8)** — same guest profile as Gulberg, more new openings per year, and the aesthetic-cafe cluster you want for category 3.
3. **Gulberg III side streets and Main Boulevard** — steakhouses and grills that aren't on MM Alam Road itself but share the same clientele.
4. **Johar Town** — younger, high-volume casual dining; good for cafés and mid-tier premium casual; slightly more price-sensitive, so lead with the "founding partner" framing (Section 6) rather than pure ROI.
5. **Bahria Town** — newer commercial areas, more new openings, less saturated with competitors' marketing tech — worth a scouting pass once you have 2-3 references from Gulberg/DHA.
6. **Model Town** — smaller but loyal premium-casual scene; good for referrals once you have a Gulberg or DHA client, since owners in Model Town often know owners elsewhere.

## 2. Decision makers — who to talk to and what they care about

| Role | What they actually care about | How to talk to them |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner** | Money in, money out. Will this bring guests back or bring new guests in? Is this a one-time cost or a recurring headache? Does it make the restaurant look more premium than competitors? | Lead with the 30-second pitch (Section 3) and the pilot price. Skip technical detail unless asked. Close on "see it once, on your own dish, on your own table" — owners decide with their eyes. |
| **General Manager (GM)** | Operations. Will staff need retraining? Will it slow down service? Will it create more work for the floor team? Does it reduce "how big is this" and "what's in this" questions from the table? | Emphasize: no app, no login, no POS integration, "show the waiter" keeps the waiter in control, works on existing wifi (built for slow connections). Show the demo on a real table, not a screen. |
| **Marketing manager** | Content and differentiation. Is this something they can post about? Does it give them a reason to talk to press or Instagram followers? Is MENVA's brand tasteful enough to sit next to theirs? | Show the brand look (cream paper, ink, chili red — calm, not cartoonish). Mention it's real 3D scans of their actual dishes, never AI-generated. Frame the founding-partner angle (Section 6) — a story they can tell. |
| **Head chef** | Food integrity. Does the 3D model actually look like the dish? Is portion size shown honestly (will it embarrass the kitchen if the real plate looks smaller)? Will this create pressure to always plate identically? | Be straight: it's a real 3D scan of an actual plated dish, true to scale, and diners can't pinch-resize it in AR — so accuracy matters and offer that the chef sees the scan before it goes live (confirm this step with Abdullah first; the scanning and review process isn't fixed yet). This is a chef's photo-realistic double, not a marketing exaggeration — say so plainly. |

In a small or mid-size restaurant, the owner is often also the GM — lead with the owner pitch and cover GM concerns (staff, wifi, service speed) in the same conversation.

## 3. The pitch

### 30 seconds (elevator / cold open)

> "We built a way for your guests to see the actual dish — not a photo, a real 3D scan — placed life-size on their own table, before they order. They scan the QR already on your table, no app, works in Safari or Chrome. Then they show the waiter what they want, so you stay in control of the order. We're running a small pilot in Lahore right now for PKR 25,000 — can I show you thirty seconds on your own table?"

**Roman Urdu version:**

> "Hum ne ek tareeqa banaya hai jahan aap ke guests apni dish ka asli 3D scan, apne hi table par, life-size dekh sakte hain — order karne se pehle. QR scan karte hain jo table par hi hai, koi app nahi chahiye, Safari ya Chrome mein hi chal jata hai. Phir woh waiter ko dikhate hain kya order karna hai, toh control aap ke paas hi rehta hai. Lahore mein abhi chhota sa pilot chala rahe hain, PKR 25,000 mein — do minute mein apne hi table par dikha sakta hoon?"

### 2 minutes (if they're listening)

> "So here's the problem we noticed: your Instagram probably gets more attention than your printed menu. Guests already want to see the food before they commit — that's why food photography works so well for you. We just moved that moment onto the table itself.
>
> A guest scans the QR you already have on the table. The menu opens right in their phone's browser — no app to download, no login. They tap a dish, and instead of a photo, they see a real 3D scan of that actual plate. On phones that support it, they can place it in AR, life-size, right on their own table — so they know exactly how big the cut is, how it's plated, before they order.
>
> It's built to work even on average restaurant wifi — the photo shows first, instantly, and the 3D model loads in behind it. And it never takes the order away from your waiter: guests build a list on their phone — dish, quantity, a note like 'no onions' — and turn the screen to show the waiter. No online ordering, no payment, nothing that goes around your staff.
>
> We track pilot analytics too — how many people scan, how many open a dish, how many use AR, how many show a list to the waiter — so you're not guessing whether it's being used.
>
> We're piloting this with a small number of restaurants in Lahore first, PKR 25,000 for the pilot. Can I show you on your own table? Takes two minutes with your phone."

### 10-minute demo script (in person, on their table)

**Setup (before you arrive):** have the pilot restaurant's live QR code loaded on your own phone as a backup demo, and, if the restaurant you're pitching has agreed in advance, their own QR/table set up. If they haven't set one up yet, demo on your own phone using the pilot restaurant's menu and narrate "this is what yours would look like."

1. **(0:00–0:30) Set the scene.** Hand them nothing yet. Say: "Before I explain anything, let me just show you." Sit at one of their own tables.
2. **(0:30–1:30) Scan.** Show the QR (yours or theirs). Scan it live with your phone, in their wifi. Let the page load in front of them — point out the dish photo appearing first, instantly, even before the 3D loads. Say: "That's on purpose — built for exactly this kind of wifi. Guests always see something beautiful right away."
3. **(1:30–3:00) Open a dish.** Tap into a hero dish (steak board or the most visual item available). Narrate the loader as it plays: "Watch — it's not a spinner, it's telling them the dish is being prepared." Let the 3D model resolve.
4. **(3:00–5:30) Place it on their own table.** This is the moment that sells. Tap "See it on your table," walk them through pointing the phone at the actual table in front of you, and let the dish appear life-size, sitting on their real tablecloth or wood grain. Say nothing for a few seconds — let them react. Then: "That's real scale. Not resized, not a trick — that's the actual plate size, right here."
5. **(5:30–6:30) Show "show the waiter."** Add the dish to the list, add a note ("medium rare" or similar), tap the floating pill, and show the full-screen high-contrast waiter view. Say: "This is what stays with you — no online ordering, no payment skimmed off, the order still comes through your waiter exactly like today."
6. **(6:30–8:00) Turn the dish, don't just place it.** If their phone doesn't support AR (or to show the fallback), rotate the dish in 3D instead. Mention: "Even on older phones this still works — it degrades gracefully, they still get something better than a photo."
7. **(8:00–9:00) Show the analytics angle.** Describe (don't fabricate numbers) what the pilot dashboard tracks: scans, dish opens, AR launches, lists shown to waiters. Say: "At the end of the pilot you'll see, in numbers, how many of your guests actually used this — not a guess."
8. **(9:00–10:00) Close.** Ask directly: "Can we do this for [their hero dish] as a pilot? It's PKR 25,000, and I'll bring the same setup I just showed you." Hand them the leave-behind (Section 7) and get a next step — a date to bring the scan, or a follow-up call.

**What to have ready on your phone before every demo:** full charge, wifi/data both working, the demo page pre-tested that morning, and the leave-behind PDF or printed page in hand so you're not fumbling for it after the "wow" moment.

**Needs Abdullah:** decide which framing(s) to actually use, confirm whether "first 5" is true at the time of any pitch, and confirm what happens at the end of the pilot before it's promised to anyone.

## 9. Pilot success-report template

### What to measure (from the analytics MENVA already tracks — see `CLAUDE.md` Phase 8)

- **Scans** — how many times a table QR was scanned (proxy for foot traffic reached).
- **Menu views** — guests who opened the menu after scanning.
- **Dish opens** — how many times a specific dish sheet was opened (tells you which dishes draw curiosity).
- **AR launches** — how many guests tried placing a dish on their table.
- **AR object placed** — how many AR sessions actually completed a placement (not just launched).
- **Model load performance** — median load time, failure rate, tier distribution (tells you if slow wifi is hurting the experience).
- **Tray adds** — how many times a dish was added to a guest's list.
- **Waiter views** — how many times a guest opened the full-screen "show the waiter" summary (the clearest signal the tool changed real ordering behavior, not just curiosity).

### How to present it to the owner (to convert pilot → paid)

1. **Open with the headline number that's actually strongest** — whichever of scans, dish opens, or AR launches has the best story for that specific restaurant. Don't force a narrative if the data doesn't support it.
2. **Show the funnel, not just totals.** Scans → menu views → dish opens → AR launches → waiter views. A funnel is more convincing than any single number because it shows the whole guest journey, and it's honest about where interest drops off.
3. **Highlight the top 2–3 dishes by opens/AR launches.** Owners respond strongly to "your guests looked at [dish] more than anything else" — it's specific and about their own menu.
4. **Be candid about weak numbers.** If scans were low, say so and discuss why (QR placement, staff not mentioning it, wifi issues) rather than spinning it. This is what earns trust for the next conversation.
5. **Compare load performance to the promise, not to a competitor.** "Dishes loaded in X seconds on your wifi" is a concrete, honest data point — never compare to a competitor's numbers you haven't measured yourself.
6. **Close with a specific next step**, not a vague "let us know." Offer a defined next-tier plan (see Section 10) with a clear scope: how many dishes, what it costs, what continues from the pilot setup.

**Never fabricate or estimate a number for this report.** If the pilot period was short or data is thin, say so plainly rather than projecting forward. `BRIEF.md` rule applies here directly: no invented stats, ever.

## 10. Referral ideas and post-pilot pricing — options for Abdullah

### Referral ideas (restaurant-to-restaurant)

- **Introduce, don't ask cold.** After a successful pilot, ask the owner: "Do you know another restaurant owner who'd want to see this?" — a warm introduction from one owner to another converts far better than any cold script above.
- **Founding-partner referral framing.** If Option A (Section 6) was used, frame referrals as "help us pick the next founding partner" — flatters the referrer and keeps the exclusivity story consistent.
- **Cross-category referrals work best.** A steakhouse owner introducing a dessert café (non-competing) is an easier ask than one competitor referring another in the same category.
- **A small referral incentive is possible** (discount on next pricing tier, or a free additional dish scan) — **needs Abdullah to decide** whether and what to offer; don't promise anything to a referring restaurant without his sign-off.

### Pricing tier ideas after the pilot — options, with reasoning

These are **ideas for Abdullah to evaluate, not commitments** — none of this should be quoted to a restaurant until he sets real numbers.

| Option | Structure idea | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Per-dish scanning fee** | Flat fee per additional dish scanned/modeled beyond the pilot set. | Simple to explain, scales naturally with how much of the menu a restaurant wants covered; easy for an owner to budget incrementally. |
| **Flat monthly/annual platform fee** | One recurring fee covering hosting, analytics dashboard, and support, regardless of dish count (within a cap). | Predictable revenue for MENVA; predictable cost for the restaurant — good for restaurants that want "set and forget." |
| **Tiered by dish count** | E.g. a "starter" tier (small number of hero dishes) vs a "full menu" tier — priced as a step function. | Matches the natural sales motion (start with hero dishes, upsell to full menu) and gives restaurants a visible upgrade path. |
| **Seasonal/menu-refresh fee** | A smaller fee each time a restaurant updates seasonal dishes and wants new scans. | Matches how restaurants actually change menus (seasonally, not continuously); keeps MENVA relevant year-round instead of a one-time sale. |
| **Analytics-only subscription add-on** | Keep the menu itself free/cheap to maintain, charge separately for the ongoing analytics dashboard. | Separates the "nice to have" (the AR menu) from the thing that proves ROI (the data) — could appeal to owners who are sold on the pilot data specifically. |

**Needs Abdullah note:** actual pricing, margins, and what a "pilot to paid" contract looks like are entirely his call — this table is only a menu of shapes to consider, not numbers to quote.
