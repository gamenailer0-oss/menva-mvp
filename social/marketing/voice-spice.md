# Voice, spiced: how MENVA stops sounding like a brochure

Abdullah's note on the first pass: "good, but feels AI generic." Fair. This page says exactly what was generic and what replaces it. Every post, caption, script and ad follows it from now on. The brand rules in `brand-book.md` still apply: real renders only, no emoji, no invented food data, Gauchos never named, and the only price is the PKR 25,000 pilot.

## 1. What made it generic

| Tell | Example from the first pass | Why it reads as AI |
|---|---|---|
| Every caption had the same shape: hook, explanation, one Roman Urdu line, then "Send this to the friend who…" | #2, #3, #7, #10, #12 | Nobody posts like that 214 times. A pattern your thumb can predict gets scrolled past. |
| The Roman Urdu was stuck on at the end, like a translation | "Kabhi kuch naya try karo." | Lahoris switch languages mid-sentence. Nobody puts a polite Urdu line on its own at the end. |
| Brochure phrasing | "Meet MENVA." "How MENVA works, in three steps." "The questions we get the most, answered." | Every startup in the world opens this way. |
| Tidy two- or three-part rhythm | "Less guessing, less waste, fewer…" "No stock photos. No AI food." | Too balanced and too polite. Real people ramble, then land one hard line. |
| Nothing specific | "a restaurant", "a menu photo", "the group" | No street names, no time of night, no real habits. That makes it sound like it could be anywhere. |
| Every joke was safe | "Every group has one." | Mild observations. No opinion, no roast, nothing anyone would argue with in the comments. |

## 2. The new voice in one line

**The friend at the table who has opinions about the menu, speaks Lahori, and roasts you lovingly.** The friend is still kind and never mean about a restaurant. But they're specific, a bit dramatic, and take a side.

## 3. Rules

1. **Say it like it's typed, not written.** Lowercase is allowed in chat-style captions. Fragments are fine. So are "yaar" and "bhai".
2. **Mix the languages mid-sentence.** Not "English sentence. Urdu sentence." Write "Friday night, 9:40, aur abhi tak koi decide nahi kar raha."
3. **Be specific: a time, a street, a habit.** For example "9:40 pm", "MM Alam", "Canal wali traffic", "3rd round of 'aur kuch?'", "the 'chef's special' nobody explains". Area and road names are fine. Never name another restaurant or brand.
4. **Take a side.** "Sharing plates are a scam if nobody tells you the size." "The safe order is a personality." Opinions get comments. Being agreeable gets scrolled past.
5. **One hard line, then stop.** Cut the explanation after the punchline. If the slide is funny, the caption can be one line.
6. **Vary the ending.** About a third of the captions have no call to action at all. Others end with a question people can't resist answering, a dare, or "tag karo". Stop using "Send this to the friend who…" on nearly every post. Use it on 1 in 10 at most.
7. **Drama over description.** "This board arrived and the table went quiet." Not "the board is larger than expected".
8. **Roast the situation, never a person, a restaurant or a waiter.** Waiters are the heroes. Menus, group chats, indecision, the uncle who orders for everyone, and our own brand are fair game.
9. **The product line stays true.** It's a QR scan with no app. It's the real plate in 3D, at true size, on your own table, before you order, and you show the waiter your list. Nothing more. No numbers we don't have.

## 4. Banned (these read as AI)

- "Meet…", "Introducing…", "Here's the thing", "Let's be honest", "Let's face it", "game-changer", "elevate", "seamless", "journey", "experience" (as a noun about MENVA), "unlock", "curated", "in today's world", "whether you're… or…".
- "Not X. It's Y." more than once per calendar.
- Lists of three short negatives ("No app. No wait. No guesswork.").
- "A short guide." "In three steps." "Answered." as closing phrases in a headline.
- Ending every caption with a save or send instruction.
- Starting a Roman Urdu line with "Ab" plus a moral.

## 5. Lahore texture bank (use it, don't overuse it)

**Moments:** Friday night 9:40 pm and still in the car park · the third "bhai aur kuch?" · the waiter hovering with the notepad · the group chat vote nobody answers · "kal se diet" · birthday dinner for 11 people · the dawat where aunty orders for the whole table · the date where nobody wants to be the one ordering "a lot" · the family that splits one of everything · after-Isha dinner rush · 1 am chai after dinner · smog-season staying in vs going out · wedding season, when everyone is too full to go out · exam season, student budget · the "Lahori portion" argument.

**People:** the one who orders last and copies you · the one who says "main kuch nahi khaun ga" and then eats your fries · the menu photographer (goes quiet, films the plate for 4 minutes) · the "yeh kya hota hai" asker · the uncle who knows the owner · the friend with a spreadsheet for the bill · the one who needs to see it before ordering (MENVA's patron saint).

**Phrases Lahoris actually type:** yaar · bhai · scene kya hai · set hai · uff · kya baat hai · bas kar · chalo · khair · pakka? · sahi hai · oye · jani · kamaal · mujhe kya pata · abhi decide karte hain · bohat hi · full · light le · "aik aur round".

**Places (areas only):** MM Alam · Gulberg · Liberty · DHA · Johar Town · Model Town · Canal Road · Fort Road side · Main Boulevard.

## 6. Formats with more energy (all in `social/templates`)

| Layout | Use it for |
|---|---|
| `chat` | A fake group chat. Made-up first names, never a real person. Best format for tagging. |
| `receipt` | A till slip where the amounts are feelings ("Regret: 1 (large)"), never prices. |
| `notes` | A phone Notes page, with struck-through items for confessions and lists. |
| `shout` | Huge stacked capitals for one punchline ("PEHLE DEKHO. PHIR ORDER."). |
| `stamp` | Any slide can carry a rubber stamp: "True size", "Not a photo", "Asli plate". |
