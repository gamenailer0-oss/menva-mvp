# MENVA — Video scripts

Companion to `reels.md` (short-form Reels) and `brand-book.md` (voice, claims policy, approved lines). This file covers the four longer-format videos MENVA needs — brand film, diner explainer, restaurant demo, founder story — plus cutdowns of each and a low-budget production plan.

Everything here follows `BRIEF.md` and `brand-book.md`:
- No invented stats, prices, client names, testimonials, dish data or results.
- The pilot restaurant is never shown or named. Scripts use generic tables and settings, or flag that a real restaurant location needs the founder's permission — never the pilot restaurant's own premises, signage, staff or logo.
- The two unconfirmed dishes are described only as **"the green plate"** and **"the trio"** (`BRIEF.md`'s own terms) — never given invented names.
- Pilot price (PKR 25,000) appears only in restaurant-facing material (the demo video and its cutdowns), never in diner-facing material, and its inclusions are never stated.
- Public-facing copy (everything spoken or shown on screen) is marked **Public**. Notes to the production team are marked **Internal — not for camera/voiceover**.
- Anything needing the founder to arrange, approve, pay for or appear in is listed under "Needs Abdullah" at the end, not done here.

## 0. Shared production notes (read before the scripts)

- **Brand look ("A · Chili"):** cream paper `#EFEBE2`, ink `#1A1714`, chili red `#B5371F`. Instrument Serif for headlines/on-screen titles, DM Sans for UI-style captions and lower thirds. Sentence case throughout, zero emoji anywhere on screen.
- **Approved lines to draw on** (from `brand-book.md` Section 3): "See it before you order it." / "See it on your table. Then order it." / Roman Urdu: "Pehle dekho, phir order." / "Order se pehle dekh lo." Use these as closing lines; don't invent new taglines inside a script.
- **What's true and sayable, and nothing else** (from `brand-book.md` Section 8): QR-to-AR menu, opens in the browser (no app), real 3D scan → AR at true size (locked, can't be pinch-resized), phones without AR can still turn the dish in 3D, photo loads first on slow wifi, "show the waiter" (add dishes + notes, no online ordering/payment), restaurants get pilot analytics (scans, dish opens, AR launches, lists shown to waiters), pilot price PKR 25,000 (restaurant-facing only, inclusions never stated), no AI-generated food ever.
- **Roman Urdu spelling:** follow the table in `brand-book.md` Section 5 (hai, hain, kya, yaar, pehle, dekho, dekh lo, khana, mangwao, karo/karna, nahi, phir, asli, bas, aana, apna/apni/apki — spelled exactly as listed there). Keep "order," "waiter," "scan," "table," "AR," "app" as English loanwords.
- **The four real assets only:** the steak board, the steak sandwich, the green plate, the trio. Every dish shown in every video is one of these four real scans, filmed either as a live AR placement (screen-recorded from `menva-ar.netlify.app`) or the finished in-app 3D model — never a photo of food that isn't one of the four, never an AI-generated or stock image.
- **How to show the product without showing the pilot restaurant:** open `menva-ar.netlify.app` directly on the phone (don't scan a QR code that's physically mounted at the pilot restaurant), and film on a neutral table — a rented/borrowed dining room, a non-pilot restaurant with its own sign-off, or a home dining table dressed plainly (no restaurant branding, no restaurant-specific crockery). This is the same working assumption `reels.md` uses in Section 6, and it applies to every script below.

## 1. Brand film (60–75 seconds)

**Use:** website hero/about section, pinned Instagram/Facebook post, opening of pitch meetings with restaurant owners or investors.
**Goal:** make the core idea — the doubt before ordering, resolved by seeing the real dish at true size — land emotionally, once, at full length. Everything else MENVA makes is a fragment of this film.

### 1.1 The story

A family or a group of old friends gather for dinner — a birthday, a reunion, an ordinary Thursday that matters because they're all in the same city at the same time for once. The table is warm, a little chaotic, very Lahori: someone's already reaching for the bread basket, someone's telling a story no one's finishing. Then the menu comes, and the room changes register for a few seconds — the specific, quiet doubt of not knowing what a dish actually looks like, whether the portion is worth splitting, whether it's the right call for a table that's waited months to eat together. One person scans/opens MENVA. The dish appears on the table, real size, right where the plate will actually go. The doubt breaks into relief — not applause, just the ordinary relief of a decision made well. They order. The film ends on the table, fuller now, the same warmth as the opening shot but resolved.

**Internal — not for camera/voiceover:** this is a mood and beat structure, not a shot-for-shot demand that every beat be literal dialogue. Directors should treat the "moment of doubt" and "relief" beats as things the camera and editing communicate — a held glance, a pause in the chatter, a laugh — rather than a character announcing "I'm not sure what to order" out loud. Keep it observational, like a documentary crew happened to be at this table, not a staged ad read.

### 1.5 Music direction (mood only — no copyrighted tracks)

**Internal — not for camera/voiceover, but shapes the final mix.**

- **Overall mood:** warm, unhurried, a little intimate — the feeling of a dinner that matters, not a product launch. Think acoustic-leaning instrumentation (a single guitar or piano figure, light strings) rather than an electronic or "corporate tech" bed. Avoid anything that reads as filmi/dramatic or as generic "uplifting startup" stock music — both fight the calm, warm, never-cartoonish brand voice.
- **Structure to score to:** near-silent/room-tone under shots 1–3 (let real table sound carry it), a very light, almost imperceptible musical bed entering around shot 4 (the doubt beat — understated, not tense or ominous), a small, warm swell (not a "drop") right at shot 8's placement moment, sustained warmth through the reaction and resolution shots, settling to near-silence again for the final card so the wordmark and tagline land in quiet.
- **Texture note for a Lahore-specific feel without cliché:** a light hand-percussion element (a soft dholak or tabla touch, used sparingly, not as the main rhythm) can localise the mood without becoming a "cultural sting." If it doesn't feel natural in the edit, leave it out entirely — a plain acoustic bed is safer than a forced local flourish.
- **Sourcing:** use a royalty-free/licensed music library (e.g. Epidemic Sound, Artlist, or Instagram/Meta's in-app licensed audio library for the cutdowns) — never a commercial track, even instrumental or "inspired by," without a confirmed licence. See Section 6.4 and "Needs Abdullah."

### 1.6 Casting and location notes

**Internal — not for camera/voiceover.**

- **Cast:** 4–6 people who read as an actual family or a long-standing friend group at a real dinner, not a casting-agency "diverse stock family." Real friends/family of the crew, or Abdullah's own circle, will read more authentically than hired talent and cost nothing — but every identifiable adult needs a signed release (Section 6.3) regardless of who they are. Mixed ages read best for "family dinner"; similar ages (late 20s–40s) read best for "friends reunion" — pick one story, don't mix both framings in the same film.
- **Direction for cast:** no scripted dialogue lines for the table guests — brief them on the beats (arriving, settling in, the menu moment, the reveal, the relief) and let them talk naturally, in whatever mix of English/Urdu/Punjabi they'd actually use. Don't feed anyone a line to say to camera; anything usable as a "quote" must be their own unprompted words, and per the claims policy, no one's words get used as a testimonial about MENVA's results — this film shows the product working, it doesn't have anyone say it worked.
- **Location:** a real dining table, warmly lit, with no restaurant branding, signage, logo-printed menus, staff uniforms or any other identifying detail in frame. Two options: (a) a home dining room, dressed simply (plain tablecloth, real crockery, candles) — needs no restaurant permission at all and is the lowest-friction option; (b) a non-pilot restaurant's dining room, borrowed for a couple of hours with that restaurant's explicit sign-off — reads slightly more "restaurant" but needs arranging (see "Needs Abdullah"). Either way: never the pilot restaurant's premises.
- **Table styling:** plain white or cream crockery, no logo-printed napkins or menus, warm practical lighting (candles, warm bulbs) rather than cool overheads. This keeps the film usable regardless of which restaurants eventually license MENVA.

### 2.3 Motion-graphics notes (matching the Chili brand)

**Internal — not for camera/voiceover.**

- **Palette, strictly:** cream `#EFEBE2` background for every title card, ink `#1A1714` for body/UI-style text, chili red `#B5371F` for numerals, underlines, progress rings and the single accent badge — never introduce a second accent colour (this mirrors `brand-book.md`'s "one accent per screen" rule).
- **Type:** Instrument Serif for the step numerals and the wordmark only; DM Sans for every other on-screen word (step labels, captions, the reinforcing line). Sentence case throughout — "Scan," "Tap," "Place," "Show the waiter" as simple labels, not "SCAN" or "Scan!".
- **Motion language:** borrow directly from the product's own loader ("The Pass," per the build spec) rather than inventing new iconography — a thin plate-rim-style progress ring, a soft crossfade between states, no bounce/elastic easing, no confetti or celebratory effects on placement (the brand voice is calm, not celebratory). Keep every transition under 0.5s so the explainer doesn't feel slower than the product itself.
- **Title cards:** identical layout each time (numeral top-left of centre, label below, chili-red underline draws in) so the four steps read as one system, not four different graphics.
- **Screen-recording capture:** record at the device's native resolution, crop to a clean phone-frame or vignette (no visible status bar clutter — hide notifications, set to airplane/DND, full battery if possible per the shared shoot checklist).

### 2.4 Captions

Burn in every caption line from the table above, DM Sans, ink text on a translucent cream bar (not a hard box) at the lower third, sized to be readable muted on a phone screen. ### 3.4 The analytics shot — how to film it honestly

**Internal — not for camera/voiceover.** This is the one shot in the whole file with real fabrication risk, so it gets its own note:

- Do **not** show the pilot restaurant's actual `/stats` numbers — that would both invent a "result" that hasn't been measured/verified for public claims and risk identifying the pilot restaurant by its data shape or timing.
- Do **not** type in made-up numbers that look like real results (e.g. "312 scans this week") — that's exactly the kind of invented stat `BRIEF.md` prohibits, even in a "demo."
- **Do** screen-record the actual `/stats` page's layout and category labels (scans / dish opens / AR launches / lists shown to waiters / tier distribution, etc.) with either (a) a clean demo/staging account showing all-zero or dashed placeholder values, or (b) the categories overlaid as motion-graphic labels on a blurred or blank dashboard shell, with an on-screen caption such as "illustrative layout — your own numbers will be different" if any placeholder values are visible at all. The point of this shot is "you get a real dashboard with these real categories," not "here are impressive numbers."

## 4. Founder story (60 seconds) — to camera, Abdullah

**Use:** pinned/feed post, "about" section, investor and press decks, the founder-on-camera reel formats already scoped in `reels.md` (R13–R15) — this is the longer, single-take version those reels get cut down from.
**Format:** **this is a prompt sheet, not a fixed script.** Abdullah answers each question in his own words, on camera, in whatever order feels natural to him. He must say only things that are true — nothing here is a line for him to read, and no answer should be edited to imply a claim he didn't actually make (no splicing words out of order to create a sentence he didn't say).

### 4.1 Questions to answer on camera

Ask these one at a time, let him answer fully before moving on, and don't interrupt an answer even if it runs long — trim in the edit, not live.

1. What made you start building MENVA? (What's the actual moment or frustration behind it — keep it specific and real, not a generic "I saw a gap in the market" line.)
2. In plain words, what does MENVA actually do?
3. Why did you decide against AI-generated food images, and insist on real scans instead?
4. What does "the waiter stays in charge" mean, and why was that important to build in on purpose?
5. What was the hardest technical or practical problem so far? ## 6. Low-budget production plan

### 6.1 Gear on a phone budget

| Item | Why | Approx. cost note |
|---|---|---|
| A recent-ish smartphone (the same phone used for the site itself works, ideally a mid-range Android and an iPhone for capturing both AR modes) | Primary camera for everything — table shots, screen recordings, to-camera pieces. | Already owned — no new spend needed if the team's existing phones are reasonable quality. |
| A small phone tripod or flexible mount | Stabilises every static shot (table-level opens, founder-to-camera) — the single highest-impact cheap upgrade. | Low cost, widely available in Lahore electronics/mobile markets. |
| A clip-on or handheld LED light (or even a second phone's flashlight bounced off a napkin/white card) | Restaurant and home dining rooms run warm and dim — a small fill light prevents grainy, muddy footage without needing a full lighting kit. | Low cost. |
| A lavalier or clip-on mic (wired, phone-compatible) — optional but recommended for the founder story and any spoken VO recording | Table/ambient audio is fine off the phone mic; a to-camera piece and narrator VO read much better with a dedicated mic. | Low-to-moderate cost; a wired lav is cheaper than wireless and sufficient for these needs. |
| A plain white or cream card/reflector (foam board, or even a large sheet of paper) | Bounces light onto faces without a second light source — useful for both the brand film table and the founder story. | Near-zero cost. |
| Storage: a few spare gigabytes free per phone before each shoot | Screen recordings and multiple takes fill storage fast. | No cost, just planning. |

Nothing above requires a professional camera, a gimbal or a lighting rig — every shot in every script here is achievable on a phone with a stand, a bit of bounce light and a clip mic.

### 6.2 Lahore shoot checklist

**Before any shoot day:**
- Confirm the location is not the pilot restaurant, and that it's either a home setting (no permission needed beyond the household's own OK) or a non-pilot restaurant with written/verbal confirmed permission from that restaurant (see "Needs Abdullah").
- Confirm every identifiable person on camera has agreed to be filmed and has seen (or will be given, on the day) the release wording in 6.3.
- Charge every phone above 80%; bring a portable battery pack/power bank as backup.
- Clear at least 5–10 GB of free storage per phone.
- Set every filming phone to Do Not Disturb / airplane mode + wifi-only during takes, so no call or notification interrupts a shot (matches `reels.md`'s own checklist).
- Clean every lens.
- Pack the tripod/mount, light, reflector card, mic, and spare cables/adapters.
- Print or have ready any printed materials needed (e.g. a plain, unbranded QR card for the explainer's "scan" shot — never a QR physically mounted at the pilot restaurant).

**On the day:**
- Shoot vertical (9:16) for every asset unless a specific horizontal cut is needed for a website embed — vertical can always be cropped for horizontal use later; the reverse isn't true.
- Lock focus and exposure (tap-and-hold on the subject/dish) before every take.
- Record 2–3 seconds of extra padding before and after every clip.
- Get every important shot (especially the AR placement "money frame" and any founder-story answer) at least twice.
- For restaurant/table shots: check the frame for any accidental restaurant branding (menus, coasters, staff uniforms, signage reflected in glass or cutlery) before rolling, every single take — this is the easiest rule to break by accident.
- For screen recordings: hide notifications, use a clean home screen, and disable auto-brightness dimming so the screen stays legible on camera.

**After the shoot:**
- Back up footage to two locations (e.g. the phone plus a laptop or cloud drive) before deleting anything.
- Log which release forms are signed/confirmed for which clips, so editors never cut in someone whose consent wasn't captured.

### 6.4 Editing apps

- **CapCut** (mobile and desktop, free tier) — the primary recommendation: has built-in captioning/auto-subtitle tools, keyframe-based motion graphics simple enough for the explainer's title cards, and a royalty-free/licensed audio library suitable for cutdowns. Good for anyone on the team without formal editing experience.

General settings for every export: H.264 codec, AAC audio, 24–30fps (match the source capture), captions burned in per 6.6 (don't rely solely on platform auto-captions, which can mis-transcribe Roman Urdu badly).

### 6.6 Subtitle style

- **Always burned in**, not relying on platform auto-generated captions — auto-captions frequently mis-transcribe Roman Urdu and brand-specific terms ("MENVA," "show the waiter").
- **Font:** DM Sans (matches the brand's UI typeface — captions should read as part of the product's own voice, not a generic caption-app font).
- **Colour:** ink `#1A1714` text; background either a translucent cream `#EFEBE2` bar (preferred, matches brand) or a simple translucent black/dark bar only if contrast against a bright background genuinely requires it.
- **Case:** sentence case, matching every other piece of MENVA copy — no ALL CAPS caption style, no emoji.
- **Placement:** lower third, sized generously enough to read at a glance on a muted phone screen (this audience will very often be watching muted), but never covering the dish or the AR placement moment itself.
- **Timing:** one short line or line-pair on screen at a time, synced tightly to speech — don't let a caption linger through two unrelated lines of VO.
- **Bilingual pieces (brand film):** since English and Roman Urdu are recorded as two separate full VO passes (Section 1.3), export two separate caption-matched versions rather than showing both languages on screen simultaneously — simultaneous bilingual captions get cluttered and undercut the "one idea per line" voice rule in `brand-book.md`.
