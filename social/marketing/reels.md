# MENVA — Reels strategy

Companion to the feed calendar. Feed posts (static, auto-generated) go out **Tue / Thu / Fri / Sat / Sun at 20:30 PKT**. This file is about Reels: real, filmed, short-form video. Everything below follows `BRIEF.md` — only confirmed MENVA facts are used, nothing is invented, the pilot restaurant is never shown or named, and "the green plate" / "the trio" are used only as the unnamed descriptors the brief itself uses, never given invented names.

All research claims below are sourced with a URL next to them, or marked **estimate**. Anything unsourced in this file is a script idea, not a fact.

---

## 1. Ten Reels principles for MENVA

1. **The first 1.5 seconds decide everything.** Reels need the visual + hook text to be legible instantly, not build up slowly — TikTok's own guidance is "hook by 1.5 seconds," and reels with a strong 3-second hold rate can out-reach weak-hold reels 5–10×. Every script below has an explicit 0–1.5s hook. ([Eliro, 2026](https://eliro.pro/blog/short-form-video-hooks-script-guide-2026); [Moonb, 2026](https://www.moonb.io/blog/instagram-reel-length))
2. **Optimise for sends and watch time, not likes.** Instagram's own ranking signals in 2026 are watch time, sends-per-reach (DMs/Stories) and saves — likes are the weakest signal. We picked a "goal" (reach / sends / saves / B2B lead) for every script so we're not guessing what a reel is for. ([TrueFuture Media, 2026](https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/instagram-reels-reach-2026-business-growth-guide); [Socialync, 2026](https://www.socialync.io/blog/instagram-shares-algorithm-complete-guide-2026))
3. **Keep most reels short, let a few run longer.** 7–15s reels tend to get the highest completion rates; 30–60s reels tend to drive more saves and shares (tutorials, myth-busting). We keep everything under 45s. ([Moonb, 2026](https://www.moonb.io/blog/instagram-reel-length))
4. **Build the loop on purpose.** Match the first and last frame, add a half-second of stillness at the very start and end — viewers often don't notice the restart, which pads watch time honestly (the reel really is being watched again). ([Vistaar WebX](https://vistaarwebx.com/the-art-of-looping-creating-seamless-reels-that-keep-viewers-watching/); [UseVisuals](https://usevisuals.com/blog/instagram-reels-hook-and-editing-tips-to-boost-watch-time))
5. **Caption everything, always on.** Most social video is watched muted for at least part of the view, and captioned reels see meaningfully longer average retention. Every script has on-screen text for every line of VO — never VO with nothing written down. ([Captions.ai, 2026](https://captions.ai/blog/how-to-make-instagram-reels); [Zebracat, 2026](https://www.zebracat.ai/post/instagram-reels-statistics))
6. **Post consistently, three times a week.** Accounts posting reels several times a week grow faster than accounts posting once or twice — we can't match influencer cadence with one founder filming, so three a week, same days, is the realistic target. ([Moonb, 2026](https://www.moonb.io/blog/instagram-reel-length))
7. **Use Instagram's own trending-audio tab at the moment of posting**, not a song picked days in advance — trends move week to week, and using flagged trending audio signals the algorithm to test the reel more widely. Never write a specific song into a script; describe the *type* of audio instead, and pick the actual track from Instagram's Trending tab (or Professional Dashboard → Trending Audio) right before publishing. ([Buffer, 2026](https://buffer.com/resources/trending-audio-instagram/); [Vista Social, 2026](https://vistasocial.com/insights/trending-audio-on-instagram/))
8. **Show the product exactly as it behaves — slow wifi and all.** MENVA is built so the dish photo shows first and the 3D follows on slow restaurant wifi. Don't edit that pause out to look "faster than real." Showing the real load sequence is honest and it's also a good hook (see the "no filming needed" reels).
9. **Never fabricate.** No invented reviews, no invented diner quotes played as real, no stats without a source, no AI-generated food imagery, no naming the pilot restaurant, no naming the two unconfirmed dishes. If a claim can't be verified, it doesn't go in a script.
10. **One CTA per reel.** Every script ends on exactly one ask — visit the site, DM to book a demo, or scan the QR in a linked Story. Never stack "follow AND visit AND comment" in one reel; competing CTAs get none of them acted on.

**Pakistan-specific note:** short-form video is now the dominant format on Pakistani social media, and Reels are increasingly used by small businesses there as a lower-cost alternative to paid ads — but there's no verified public benchmark for restaurant/food-tech reels specifically in Lahore, so we're not claiming one. Treat every performance number in this file as an industry-wide estimate, not a Pakistan-specific or MENVA-specific guarantee. ([Influence of Instagram Reels on Marketing Tactics of Small Businesses: Insights from Pakistan, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381776325_Influence_of_Instagram_Reels_on_Marketing_Tactics_of_Small_Businesses_Insights_from_Pakistan))

## 2. Thirty Reel scripts

Format key used below — **Goal**: reach / sends / saves / B2B lead. **Hook** is exactly what appears on screen and is said in the first 1.5 seconds. **Needed** is what production requires; anything needing another person's face, another restaurant, or a stranger goes to the "Needs Abdullah" list in Section 6 — don't arrange it without him.

Dish references use only what `BRIEF.md` confirms: **the steak board**, **the steak sandwich**, **the green plate**, **the trio** (the last two are the brief's own unnamed descriptors — never invent a dish name for them).

### A. POV

**R1. POV: you just sat down and you're starving**
Goal: reach · Length: 10s · Needed: a restaurant or home table, one phone, one friend's hands (or Abdullah's own)
Hook (0–1.5s): On-screen "POV: you just sat down" / VO: "Bethte hi sabse pehle ye karo."
Shots:
1. 0–2s — Hands placing phone on table, opening the site directly (menva-ar.netlify.app — not scanning at the pilot restaurant). On-screen: "POV: you just sat down." VO: "Bethte hi sabse pehle ye karo."
2. 2–5s — Dish photo of the steak board loads instantly on screen. On-screen: "the photo loads first." VO: "Photo turant aa jaati hai."
3. 5–8s — 3D model spins in, then AR button appears. On-screen: "then the real 3D." VO: "Phir asli 3D."
4. 8–10s — Phone tilts, dish appears placed on the table at true size, loop back to shot 1 framing. On-screen: "see it before you order it." VO: "Order se pehle dekh lo."
Audio: a low, hungry/anticipation trend sound (check Trending tab) or plain ambient restaurant room tone.
Caption: "See it before you order it. No app, just your camera."
Hashtags: #MENVA #LahoreFood #SeeItBeforeYouOrder #ARdining #LahoreEats
CTA: "Link in bio to try it yourself."

## 5. Filming checklist and phone setup guide

### Before every shoot
- Phone charged above 50%, storage has at least 5 GB free.
- Do Not Disturb / Airplane mode + wifi-only, so no call or notification interrupts a take.
- Clean the lens.
- Shoot vertical (9:16), lock focus/exposure by tap-and-hold on the subject before rolling.
- Record 2–3 seconds of extra padding before and after every clip — makes editing and looping much easier.
- Get every take at least twice.
- For any reel with another person's face: get their spoken OK on camera before filming, or confirmed separately beforehand (see Section 6).

### Lighting in restaurants
- Fine-dining spaces are usually warm and dim — sit near a window or any natural light source if one is available.
- Avoid shooting with a bright window or overhead light directly behind the subject (backlighting silhouettes the face).
- Tap-and-hold on the phone screen over the dish/face to lock exposure and white balance before recording, so the shot doesn't hunt for focus mid-take.
- Skip the phone's flash — it's harsh and flattens the food. A small clip-on LED or even a phone's flashlight bounced off a napkin works better in low light.
- White balance in most restaurants runs warm (orange/yellow bulbs) — locking exposure on the dish itself (not the background) usually corrects for this automatically.

### Stabilizing
- A small tripod or a phone stand (even propped against a water glass or menu) beats handheld for anything longer than 3 seconds.
- For walking/POV shots, hold the phone at chest height with both hands and take slow, small steps.
- For the AR placement shots specifically, keep the phone as still as possible for the first second after the dish appears — that's the "money frame" that gets used as the poster/thumbnail.

### Screen-recording the AR demo — iPhone
1. Settings → Control Center → add "Screen Recording" if it isn't already there.
2. Open Control Center, tap the Screen Recording icon, wait for the 3-second countdown.
3. Open Safari, go to the site, run through the flow normally — Quick Look AR is captured directly by the recording.
4. To add live narration, long-press the Screen Recording icon before starting and turn the microphone on.
5. Tap the red status bar (or the Screen Recording icon again) to stop.

### Screen-recording the AR demo — Android
1. Swipe down twice for Quick Settings and look for the "Screen Record" tile (built into Android 11+); if it's missing, add it via the pencil/edit icon, or use the phone brand's own screen recorder app (Samsung, Xiaomi, etc. all ship one).
2. Choose whether to include the microphone for narration before starting.
3. Open Chrome, go to the site, run the flow — Scene Viewer/WebXR AR is captured directly.
4. Some Android skins restrict recording during camera-permission overlays — test the exact phone model once before a real shoot, since behaviour varies by manufacturer.
5. Stop from the notification shade or the floating recorder control.
