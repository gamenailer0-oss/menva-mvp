## 1. TikTok Pakistan

### 1.1 Status and legal stability in 2026

TikTok is currently accessible in Pakistan, but its legal footing is not stable and should not be treated as permanent. Pakistan has banned the app **five times since 2020**, each time initiated by the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) or by court order over "immoral" or "unethical" content, with the platform restored once some compliance was confirmed. ([BitJoy — TikTok Pakistan Ban: Current Status, Reasons & Full Timeline](https://thebitjoy.com/blogs/blog/tiktok-pakistan-ban-what-happened-why-it-keeps-happening-and-the-current-status))

The legal basis for any ban is the Pakistan Electronic Crimes Act (PECA); the PTA can restrict or block a platform under PECA and lift the block once it judges the platform compliant. ([BitJoy](https://thebitjoy.com/blogs/blog/tiktok-pakistan-ban-what-happened-why-it-keeps-happening-and-the-current-status))

As of September 2026 there are live, ongoing legal threats, not a settled situation:
- A petition filed in June 2026 asks a court to direct authorities to permanently ban TikTok for "continued, recurring and consistent breach" of Pakistan's constitution and other laws. ([BitJoy](https://thebitjoy.com/blogs/blog/tiktok-pakistan-ban-what-happened-why-it-keeps-happening-and-the-current-status))
- A separate petition before the Islamabad High Court seeks to restrict TikTok, Instagram and Facebook for users under 16. ([BitJoy](https://thebitjoy.com/blogs/blog/tiktok-pakistan-ban-what-happened-why-it-keeps-happening-and-the-current-status))
- A government proposal has also been reported to restrict Facebook, TikTok and Instagram specifically for children. ([Daily Pakistan — Pakistan moves to ban Facebook, TikTok, Instagram for children](https://en.dailypakistan.com.pk/24-Aug-2026/pakistan-moves-to-ban-facebook-tiktok-instagram-for-children-full-details-here))

**What this means for MENVA:** treat TikTok as a real, active channel, but never as the only short-form video channel. Every TikTok video should also exist as an Instagram Reel (this already happens per the Reels strategy), so a sudden PTA block does not zero out MENVA's short-form video presence overnight. Do not build any MENVA feature (QR redirect, analytics link) that depends on TikTok being reachable.

This is platform self-reported ad-reach data, not independently audited — treat as directional. ([DataReportal — Digital 2026: Pakistan](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-pakistan))

### 1.4 Repurposing MENVA's Reels for TikTok

Do not simply re-upload the Instagram Reel export.
1. **Always start from the clean master file**, exported without Instagram's or CapCut's watermark, and post that same clean file to TikTok. Both Instagram and YouTube (and, by the same logic, TikTok's own ranking systems) are reported to de-prioritise or suppress reach on videos carrying a visible competitor-platform watermark. ([Slidy Creator — Cross-posting without watermark penalties](https://slidycreator.com/blog/cross-posting-without-watermarks/); [SyncStudio — Cross-post business videos in 2026: the suppression rules](https://syncstudio.ai/blog/cross-post-business-videos-2026))
2. **Re-cut the hook for TikTok's faster scroll speed.** Keep the same footage and VO from the Reels script, but trim any lead-in before the hook line lands — TikTok viewers judge in the first 1–2 seconds, same discipline the Reels strategy already applies.
3. **Swap the caption's slang register slightly.** Reels captions already use one English hook + one Roman Urdu line (per `research/genz-culture.md`); keep that exact discipline on TikTok too — don't add extra slang just because "it's TikTok."
4. **Re-pick trending audio per platform, at time of posting**, the same rule the Reels strategy already sets — TikTok and Instagram trending sounds are not always the same sound in the same week.
5. **Use TikTok-native captions/hashtags**, not a copy-pasted Instagram hashtag block — see the starter plan below for concrete tag sets.
6. **Never post the pilot restaurant's name, premises identifiers, or the two unconfirmed dish names** — same rule as every other channel.

### 2.2 Broadcast-list strategy for restaurant leads (opt-in only)

WhatsApp Business app broadcast lists are capped at **256 saved contacts per list**, and a broadcast only reaches a contact if they have MENVA's number saved in their own phone. ([Respond.io — WhatsApp Business app: the ultimate guide](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-business-app); [SetSmart — WhatsApp broadcast 2026: limits, tools, templates](https://setsmart.io/blog/whatsapp-broadcast))

Rules, straight from current best-practice research — **opt-in only, no exceptions**:
- Only add a restaurant contact to a broadcast list after they have messaged first, given a business card with explicit "message me on WhatsApp" intent, or verbally agreed at an event/meeting to be messaged — never add a number scraped from a Google listing, a group, or a directory. Spam reports are the primary trigger for account restriction. ([Blueticks — WhatsApp campaign best practices: 12 rules for 2026](https://blueticks.co/blog/whatsapp-campaign-best-practices); [ChatbotX — How to broadcast messages without getting banned, 2026](https://chatbotx.io/blog/how-to-broadcast-messages-on-whatsapp-without-getting-banned-2026-guide/))
- **Frequency: 1–3 broadcasts a week maximum** to any one list — more than that measurably increases fatigue and opt-outs. ([Blueticks](https://blueticks.co/blog/whatsapp-campaign-best-practices))
- **Segment the list**, don't blast everyone the same message — e.g. a separate send for "restaurants that saw a demo but haven't decided" vs. "restaurants that haven't seen a demo yet." Sending the same generic message to the whole list every time is what gets reported as spam. ([Blueticks](https://blueticks.co/blog/whatsapp-campaign-best-practices))
- **Always include an easy opt-out** — a line like "Reply STOP if you'd rather not get these updates" measurably reduces block/report rates. ([Blueticks](https://blueticks.co/blog/whatsapp-campaign-best-practices))
- **Watch opt-out rate per send.** If it spikes, stop broadcasting to that segment, work out why, and fix the message before sending again. ([Blueticks](https://blueticks.co/blog/whatsapp-campaign-best-practices))
- Suggested list structure once MENVA has enough contacts to need it: `Warm — saw a demo`, `Warm — inquired, no demo yet`, `Pilot restaurant / active`, `Cold — met once, opted in`. Each stays well under the 256-contact cap.

Sample opt-in-respecting broadcast message (ready to paste, use sparingly, 1–3×/week max):

> "Hi — quick MENVA update: [what changed, e.g. 'we just finished our first pilot install' or 'new short demo video, 40 seconds']. Reply if you'd like to see it, or reply STOP if you'd rather not get these."

### 2.4 Restaurant-group etiquette

Pakistani hospitality/restaurant-owner WhatsApp groups exist but membership is invitation- or referral-based, so this is a "Needs Abdullah" access point (Section 8), not something to join anonymously. Once in a group:
- **Give value before pitching.** Answer questions, share genuinely useful info (e.g. something relevant to running a restaurant), before ever mentioning MENVA.
- **Never cold-pitch the group as a whole.** A single unsolicited "check out my product" message in a group of restaurant owners reads as spam and can get a member removed — the same spam-report logic that governs broadcast lists (Section 2.2) applies socially in a group too.
- **Only mention MENVA when directly relevant** — e.g. someone asks about menu presentation, QR ordering, or AR/tech in dining — and even then, keep it to one or two sentences and invite a DM rather than pitching in the group thread.
- **Never post a group member's restaurant name, photo, or any detail from the group publicly** without that person's explicit permission — groups are semi-private spaces and screenshotting/reposting group content elsewhere is a real etiquette violation, per general Facebook/WhatsApp group norms. ([MooseStudio — Facebook group etiquette rules](https://moosestudio.com/socialmedia/7-rules-etiquette-facebook-groups/))
- **Respond to DMs that come out of group visibility promptly and personally** — this is where actual leads convert, not the group thread itself.

### 3.2 Twenty ready LinkedIn posts

Each has a hook (first line, what shows before "see more"), the story, and the lesson. All use placeholders for any number. None invent a result, a client name, or a testimonial.

**1. The problem MENVA solves**
Hook: "Every restaurant menu makes a promise it can't keep."
Story: A menu photo is never quite what arrives at the table — angle, lighting, portion size, all slightly off. In Lahore specifically, dining out is social infrastructure — family dawats, friend hangouts — and the moment of ordering blind is a small but real source of friction every time.
Lesson: The gap between "what the menu shows" and "what actually arrives" is the whole reason MENVA exists. Closing that gap honestly, without tricks, turned out to be harder — and more interesting — than I expected.

**2. Why MENVA, in plain words**
Hook: "MENVA lets you see your dish, at true size, on your own table, before you order it."
Story: A diner scans a QR code at their table. No app, no download — it opens straight in Safari or Chrome. They tap a dish and see a real 3D scan of the plate. Then they can place it on their own table in AR, at true size.
Lesson: The idea only works if every piece of it is real — real scans, real size, no shortcuts. That constraint shaped almost every decision after it.

**3. Why we never use AI-generated food images**
Hook: "MENVA will never show you a dish that doesn't exist."
Story: It would be easy to fill gaps with AI-generated food photography — faster, cheaper, no scanning equipment needed. We ruled it out from day one.
Lesson: The entire premise of MENVA is "see it before you order it." An AI-generated image breaks that promise the first time a diner notices the food doesn't quite look real. Trust, once broken, is expensive to rebuild — so we just never risk it.

**4. Why there's no online ordering**
Hook: "MENVA doesn't take your order. Your waiter still does."
Story: It would have been a natural next step to add checkout, payments, order submission. We deliberately didn't.
Lesson: "Show the waiter" — a diner builds a list on their phone, adds notes like "no onions," and turns the screen to their server. The waiter stays in charge of the table. Some products get better by doing less, not more.

**5. What slow restaurant wifi taught me about product design**
Hook: "The best feature in MENVA might be what happens when the wifi is bad."
Story: Restaurant wifi in Lahore is not always fast or stable. Early on, that felt like a constraint to work around. It became a design principle instead.
Lesson: The dish photo always loads first. The 3D model follows once it can. A diner never stares at a blank screen wondering if something broke — they see a beautiful photo of the dish immediately, and the fuller experience catches up.

**6. The first pilot**
Hook: "We're running our first pilot with a restaurant in Lahore."
Story: [Detail Abdullah is comfortable sharing publicly — restaurant name withheld]. The pilot covers table QR codes, the AR dish menu, and analytics on scans, dish opens, AR launches, and how often a diner's list gets shown to the waiter.
Lesson: [number] weeks in, here's what I've learned so far: [Abdullah to fill in with real, verifiable detail only].

**7. Why true size, locked**
Hook: "In MENVA's AR view, you can't resize the dish. That's on purpose."
Story: Most AR viewers let you pinch to scale an object up or down. We turned that off.
Lesson: The whole point of true-size AR is that it's checkable — you can hold your hand next to it, compare it to your actual plate. Letting someone resize it for a "better view" would quietly undo the one thing that makes it trustworthy.

**8. Building for Lahore, not for a demo reel**
Hook: "A lot of AR product demos are built for a conference stage, not a real restaurant table."
Story: Restaurant wifi, mid-range Android phones, restaurant lighting, restaurant noise — none of that is present in a polished product demo, and all of it is present in the actual use case.
Lesson: MENVA is built and tested against the real conditions it will be used in, not the conditions that make a demo look impressive. That's a slower way to build, and I think it's the only honest way to build this.

**9. The hardest part wasn't the AR**
Hook: "Getting a 3D model into AR is, at this point, a solved problem. Getting people to trust it wasn't."
Story: The technical pieces — scanning, model placement, browser-based AR — all have existing tools. The harder problem was making sure a diner's first reaction is "that's real," not "is this CGI?"

## 6. YouTube Shorts — repurposing rules

1. **Never upload a file carrying a TikTok or CapCut watermark.** YouTube's Shorts recommendation system is reported to de-prioritise content with a visible other-platform watermark, the same penalty pattern described for Instagram Reels. Always re-export and upload the clean master file. ([Slidy Creator — Post one video to TikTok, Reels and Shorts without watermark penalties](https://slidycreator.com/blog/cross-posting-without-watermarks/))
2. **Keep the same 9:16 vertical framing** used for Reels/TikTok — no re-cropping needed if the master file was shot vertically from the start.
3. **Re-check pacing, not just resolution.** YouTube Shorts audiences are reported to tolerate a slightly slower build than TikTok's fastest-scroll audience, but the practical rule for MENVA is simple: keep the same sub-2-second hook discipline used everywhere else — it never hurts, and it's one less thing to vary per platform.
4. **Add the `#Shorts` tag** and a clear, factual title (e.g. "Real 3D dish scan, no AI — MENVA") — Shorts titles function more like search results than captions, unlike TikTok/Reels captions.
5. **Burn in captions**, same as every other channel — muted viewing is the default across all short-form platforms.
6. **Link back to menva-ar.netlify.app** in the video description every time — YouTube descriptions are the one place a clickable link is reliably available without a "link in bio" workaround.
7. **Post the same 3 weekly clips already filmed for Reels/TikTok** — no separate YouTube-only production. This is a distribution channel, not a new content workload.
8. **Every other brief rule still applies**: no AI food imagery, no naming the pilot restaurant, no inventing the two unconfirmed dish names, no fabricated numbers in the description or comments.
