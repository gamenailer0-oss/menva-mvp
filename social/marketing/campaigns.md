## Campaign 1 — Sight Test

**One-line idea:** The taste-test reaction format, flipped — people guess a dish's size from its plain menu name, then watch it prove them right or wrong at true size in AR.

### The insight
`research/pakistan-food-trends.md` mechanic #4 explicitly reframes taste-test/reaction culture (Hamza Bhatti, Saqib Mobeen and other Pakistani food vloggers' reaction-review format) into a "sight-test": MENVA can't offer taste, but it can offer the equivalent of seeing the AR-placed dish next to the real one, honestly, before ordering. `research/competitors.md` section 3 adds the mechanism why this works: the one solid, peer-reviewed field result found anywhere in this research (Fritz, Hadi & Stephen, *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, 2023 — one UK restaurant, dessert menu only, 41.2% vs 18% order rate) attributes AR's effect to "mental simulation" of the real thing — closing exactly the size/portion uncertainty a menu line can't answer. That study is about someone else's dessert menu in another country and must never be quoted as a MENVA result; it only explains *why* a guess-then-reveal format should work.

### The mechanic, and why it gets sends/saves/comments
Step one shows only the plain dish name — no photo, no size cue — and asks the audience to guess how big it actually is (in felt terms: fits a palm / fills a plate / takes the table — never a fabricated number or cm figure, since plate sizes aren't confirmed per `CLAUDE.md`). Step two reveals the real AR placement at true size. Guessing is an easy, low-effort way to comment (people post their guess before scrolling on), and being wrong (or right) about a food's size is the kind of small, real surprise people send to a friend to settle an argument — the same energy as the taste-test genre, without needing taste.

### Executions

**Instagram feed post** (carousel, ready to paste)
- Slide 1 (text only, cream card): "Guess the size."
- Slide 2 (text only): "the steak sandwich" *(swap in the featured dish name each time; never invent a name for the unconfirmed two — use "the green plate" / "the trio")*
- Slide 3: real poster image, close crop.
- Slide 4: AR placement screenshot at true size, on a neutral, non-branded table.
- Caption: "Guess the size before you scan. Comment your guess — palm, plate, or table? Andaza laga lo, phir scan kar ke dekh lo. See it on your table. Then order it."
- Hashtags: `#menva #lahorefood #lahorefoodies`

**Story:** poll sticker — "How big is the steak sandwich? A) Fits your palm B) Fills your plate C) Takes the table" — reveal the AR screenshot in the next frame.

**Reel (script summary, 12–15s):** hands miming a size guess over a plain-text menu card → cut to a phone lifting → AR dish settles on a real table at true size → a beat on the reaction → ends on the chili-red ring closing + wordmark, per the brand's recurring sign-off device.

**TikTok:** same cut, native pacing (slightly faster jump cut into the reveal), captioned with the guess options on-screen.

**WhatsApp:** short broadcast text + the reveal clip: "Guess the size before you scan. Reply with your guess." *(needs a broadcast list — see Needs Abdullah.)*

**Offline / at the partner restaurant:** a small table card next to the QR stand — "Guess the size. Scan to find out." — needs the restaurant's sign-off to place.

**PR hook:** pitch a local food/lifestyle or tech writer on "the first Lahore menu where you can literally see the size of your order before you order it" as a novelty-tech angle, without naming or depicting [pilot restaurant] unless it agrees to be credited.

### Partner needs
Runs **today** with existing assets: the guess-and-reveal format uses only the four existing scans and a screen recording of the live product filmed on the team's own table (not at the restaurant, no restaurant branding visible) — no new scan, no restaurant permission needed for the online executions. The **offline table card and any PR that names or is filmed at [pilot restaurant]** needs Abdullah and restaurant sign-off.

### Timeline and budget
- Weeks 1–4: one dish per week, doubling as Dish Drop's own weekly reveal (see Campaign 2) so production effort is shared.
- Ongoing: repeat monthly or when a new dish scan exists.
- Budget: PKR 0 (in-house filming/editing) to PKR 5,000–25,000 if a nano-influencer is paid to film their own guess-reaction version (rate per `research/competitors.md` section 6).

### KPIs and kill criterion
KPIs: comments (guesses), sends per reach, saves, DMs "PILOT" from any restaurant owner who engages. Kill criterion: if four posts in this format show no lift in comments or sends over the account's normal per-post baseline for two consecutive weeks, drop the weekly slot to quarterly rather than retiring it outright.

### Risks and cringe check
Risk: repeating a "gotcha" tone that makes someone feel silly for guessing wrong — the reveal must read as delightful, not a correction. Risk: implying a specific cm/size figure that isn't confirmed data. Cringe check: keep the guess options plain and Lahori ("palm, plate, table"), no forced slang stacked into one caption (per `research/genz-culture.md`'s own "don't stack more than one slang term" rule).

## Campaign 2 — Dish Drop

**One-line idea:** A Crumble-style weekly reveal — one real scanned dish gets the spotlight each week, "this week on the table."

### The insight
`research/pakistan-food-trends.md` section 3, mechanic #1: Crumble Pakistan's rotating weekly cookie flavours (six-day availability windows) are cited as "the single biggest lever" in the whole research set — scarcity plus a reveal, where the reveal itself is the content, not an ad (Aurora, Social Samosa, Slurry Digital, Runway Pakistan sources cited there). The same file's own MENVA adaptation already names this exact idea: "there are only four scans that exist, so featuring them one at a time, in rotation, is simply accurate, not manufactured hype." This campaign builds that idea out into a full, repeatable series.

### The mechanic, and why it gets sends/saves/comments
Each week, one of the four dishes is the featured "drop" across the account's channels, opening with the same blurred-photo-resolving-into-focus beat as the product's own "The Pass" loader (`CLAUDE.md` section 6) — a legitimate unboxing moment borrowed from the product itself, not invented for marketing. Because there are genuinely only four scans, the honest framing is that the *spotlight* rotates weekly, not that the *dish* is limited-time at the restaurant (a distinction this campaign must hold precisely — see Risks). Comments will include skepticism ("is this CGI?"); replying calmly and consistently, with a link to the actual scan, is MENVA's version of the chai-wars-style public banter `research/pakistan-food-trends.md` describes (Tapal vs Lipton, Crumble vs Blinkit) — not brand-vs-brand, but brand-vs-doubt, giving the audience a side to root for ("real scan" vs "just CGI") without picking a fight with anyone.

### Executions

**Instagram feed post** (carousel, ready to paste)
- Slide 1: "This week on the table: the steak sandwich."
- Slide 2: poster image, close crop.
- Slide 3: AR placement screenshot at true size.
- Slide 4: "Real scan. No AI. No filter."
- Caption: "This week on the table: the steak sandwich. Scan it, place it at true size on your own table, then decide. Is hafte ki dish yeh hai — order se pehle dekh lo. See it on your table. Then order it."
- Hashtags: `#menva #lahorefood #lahorefoodies`

**Story:** day-before teaser — the poster shown at `blur(18px)`, same focus-pull effect as the in-product loader, with the line "Tomorrow: this week's drop."

**Reel (script summary, 10–15s):** blurred poster resolving to sharp focus → cut to AR placement on a table → close on one scale detail (a fork, a hand) → sign-off ring.

**TikTok:** same cut with an on-screen counter, "week 1 of 4," so the rotation itself reads as a series worth following.

**WhatsApp:** a weekly broadcast line to any opted-in list *(needs Abdullah — see below)*.

**Offline / at the partner restaurant:** a small "on the table this week" card near the QR stand — needs restaurant sign-off.

**PR hook:** once a few weeks of drops exist, pitch "Lahore's first weekly AR dish rotation" as a novelty story, sharing only real Instagram Insights numbers once available and with Abdullah's OK — never invented figures.

### Partner needs
The **core weekly reveal runs today** — four existing scans, existing website, team-filmed screen recordings, no restaurant access required. **Needs partner:** the offline table card, and any copy or PR that explicitly ties a given week's drop to being physically served at [pilot restaurant] right now.

### Timeline and budget
- Weeks 1–4: one dish per week (steak board → steak sandwich → green plate → trio) — an honest first "season."
- Week 5 on: repeat the same four with a new angle (paired with other pillars, e.g. "types of people who'd order this"), or slow to fortnightly to avoid repetition fatigue.
- Budget: PKR 0 — existing assets, in-house production.

### KPIs and kill criterion
KPIs: sends per reach (primary — scarcity-style formats are built to be forwarded "before it rotates"), saves, comments (including "is this real" replies as a tracked engagement signal), DMs "PILOT". Kill criterion: if sends per reach for the Dish Drop slot falls below the account's own trailing baseline (its average across the automated calendar's "see it first" pillar) for three consecutive weeks, drop the weekly cadence to monthly and revisit the format.

### Risks and cringe check
Risk: only four real dishes exist — repeating them signals thin pilot inventory once regulars notice; the caption must never imply the *dish itself* is scarce or limited-time at the restaurant, only that this week's *spotlight* is on it. If asked "is this all you have," reply honestly ("four real dishes are scanned so far, more as the pilot grows") rather than deflect. Cringe check: no manufactured urgency ("hurry," fake countdowns) — stay factual and calm, matching the brand's stated voice.

## Campaign 3 — Menu Photos Lie

**One-line idea:** Expectation vs reality, done honestly — MENVA never fabricates a "bad" comparison photo and never mocks a named restaurant; it only ever shows its own real scan as the honest answer to a universal frustration.

### The insight
`research/pakistan-food-trends.md`'s case table lists "expectation vs reality" as a globally recognised comedic food format (Know Your Meme), explicitly flagged there as having **no verified Pakistan-specific viral instance** — included, in the research's own words, "because it is directly relevant to MENVA's value proposition," not because it's a confirmed local trend. Section 4, trend-jacking idea #3, sketches the exact adaptation this campaign builds out: a text-based callout of the universal problem, next to MENVA's own real scan — never a fabricated "before" photo, which would itself break the brief's no-AI-imagery, no-invented-data rules.

### The mechanic, and why it gets sends/saves/comments
Instead of showing any specific bad photo (real or fabricated — both are off-limits: fabricating one breaks the AI-imagery rule, and using a real one would mean naming or shaming a business), the campaign names the shared, near-universal feeling in text only — "we've all ordered off a photo and got something else" — then answers it with MENVA's own real scan and true-size AR placement. This is a save-and-send format almost by design: it states a frustration everyone recognizes (which is exactly why `research/pakistan-food-trends.md` flags the format as one that "doesn't need a specific brand to work") and resolves it with something genuinely useful to reference before the next time someone orders.

### Executions

**Instagram feed post** (carousel, ready to paste)
- Slide 1 (text only): "We've all ordered off a photo and got something else."
- Slide 2 (text only): "No more expectation vs reality."
- Slide 3: real scan poster + AR placement screenshot.
- Caption: "The gap between the photo and the plate — closed. This is a real 3D scan, placed at true size on your own table, before you order. Photo aur plate ka farq — khatam. Order se pehle asli size dekh lo. See it on your table. Then order it."
- Hashtags: `#menva #lahorefood #lahorefoodies`

**Story:** an open invite sticker asking followers to DM their own "expectation vs reality" food story (their words, their consent) to be turned into a credited, anonymized text-quote card later — never their photo of a specific dish, and never without asking first.

**Reel (script summary):** a quick montage of generic, non-brand-specific text cards stating the universal complaint (no real dish other than MENVA's own is ever shown), then a cut to MENVA's real scan/AR reveal, ending on the sign-off.

**TikTok:** a stitch-friendly version inviting people to duet with their *own* expectation-vs-reality stories; MENVA's own clip stays entirely about its own scan and never depicts anyone else's dish or restaurant.

**WhatsApp:** the same graphic card, ready to forward.

**Offline / at the partner restaurant:** a small table card — "What you ordered is what you saw. Every time." — needs restaurant sign-off, since it associates MENVA's honesty positioning with that specific venue.

**PR hook:** pitch the general "menu photo trust problem" to a Lahore lifestyle or tech writer, citing the format as a *global* genre (per the research's own caveat that no Pakistani case was verified) — never oversold as a documented local trend. Needs Abdullah's sign-off before outreach.

### Partner needs
Runs **today**: text-plus-real-scan executions across IG/Reel/TikTok/WhatsApp use no restaurant imagery and no fabricated comparison photo. **Needs partner:** the offline table card, and any PR that associates the campaign's honesty positioning with [pilot restaurant] specifically.

### Timeline and budget
- Weeks 2–3: run for two weeks, starting a week after Dish Drop so the audience already has some scan-familiarity.
- After that: evergreen, occasional repost (once every 3–4 weeks), not a constant drumbeat — the message lands best delivered a few times, not repeated to exhaustion.
- Budget: PKR 0 in-house; optional PKR 5,000–25,000 for a nano-influencer collab sharing their own story (rate per `research/competitors.md`).

### KPIs and kill criterion
KPIs: saves (this is a useful/relatable format, high-save potential per `social/content/plan.md`'s own pillar logic), sends, comments (people sharing their own bad-photo story), DMs "PILOT". Kill criterion — a **brand-risk kill, not a metrics kill**: if comments skew toward people naming specific real restaurants and MENVA is pulled into a public callout it didn't intend, pause the format immediately and moderate comments, regardless of reach performance.

### Risks and cringe check
This is the highest-risk campaign of the six: commenters will very likely name real restaurants unprompted ("this happened to me at X") — MENVA must never amplify, quote, endorse or reply in a way that appears to pile on a named business. Never fabricate a "bad photo" of any kind. Cringe check: avoid the word "lie" anywhere in public copy (it stays an internal codename only); tone must read as generous and useful, not smug — no "gotcha, other menus lie to you" framing.

## Campaign 4 — Founding Tables

**One-line idea:** B2B — the first Lahore restaurants to put their menu on the table, framed as a founding-member option for Abdullah to offer, not a cold pitch.

### The insight
`research/competitors.md` section 6 shows PKR 25,000 for the whole MENVA pilot reads as roughly one micro-influencer post's worth of spend (PKR 25,000–150,000), or close to a third of what the closest real overseas comparable would cost for four dishes at its own published rate (ARmenu, from £49/dish, ~PKR 74,000 for four dishes at September 2026's approximate rate — the document's own estimate, not sourced, and explicitly "illustrative, not a line-item comparison"). Section 5 adds that no AR-menu competitor operates in Pakistan at all, and that real 3D scanning — not the AR delivery layer — is the genuine bottleneck for every competitor reviewed, which means MENVA's onboarding capacity really is limited early on. `research/pakistan-food-trends.md` mechanic #7 (founder-on-camera credibility, Crumble's GIKI-student founders) is the B2B trust-building parallel: restaurant owners trust a plain-spoken founder explanation over a sales pitch.

### The mechanic, and why it gets DMs/comments
Reframes the pilot fee from "buying software" to "one of a genuinely small number of founding restaurants shaping what this becomes in Lahore" — an honest scarcity, since MENVA's actual scanning/onboarding capacity is small by nature of the product, not manufactured. A plain, fact-only post removing the biggest B2B friction (unclear pricing, unclear mechanics) reads as more trustworthy to an owner than a polished pitch deck, matching plan.md's own existing "Owners ask" myth-busting pillar.

### Executions

**Instagram feed post** (restaurant-facing, ready to paste)
- Slide 1: "The first Lahore restaurants on MENVA."
- Slide 2: "Lahore pilot: PKR 25,000. Real 3D scans. No app for your diners. Pilot analytics: scans, dish opens, AR launches, lists shown to waiters."
- Slide 3: "DM 'PILOT' to be one of the first."
- Caption: "We're looking for the first Lahore restaurants to put their menu on the table — real 3D, true size, no app. Lahore pilot: PKR 25,000. DM 'PILOT' to find out more for your restaurant. Apna menu apni table par laao — sabse pehle. DM karo 'PILOT'."
- Hashtags: `#menva #lahorerestaurants #restaurantmarketing`

**Story:** a DM-sticker Q&A aimed at owners/managers ("ask us anything about the pilot"). Public answers stay strictly to confirmed facts from `BRIEF.md`; any question about what PKR 25,000 includes is answered with "DM us, we'll walk you through it" — never an invented public answer, since inclusions are explicitly not known.

**Reel (script summary):** a calm, text-card-only walkthrough of the known pilot steps (scan the QR → real 3D → AR at true size → show the waiter → restaurant gets pilot analytics), no voiceover needed, ending "DM 'PILOT'." No ROI or uplift claims — `research/competitors.md` is explicit that the one solid AR-sales study found (112% dessert spend increase) is someone else's UK dessert menu, not a MENVA result, and must never be borrowed as one.

**TikTok:** a shorter cut of the same reel.

**WhatsApp:** a short, factual one-pager (same facts as the IG slides) sent by Abdullah to his own restaurant-owner contacts — this is Abdullah's direct outreach, not a brand broadcast.

**Offline / at the partner restaurant:** in-person pitch meetings using the same fact card, printed or on a tablet — this leg is entirely Abdullah's.

**PR hook:** once at least one founding restaurant is signed, pitch a short "why we're calling the first restaurants Founding Tables" founder-story angle to a Lahore startup/business outlet — needs Abdullah on record and any restaurant named only with its consent.

### Partner needs
The IG/Reel/TikTok fact-card content **runs today** — it uses only the four existing scans generically and facts already confirmed in `BRIEF.md`, no restaurant-specific imagery. **Needs Abdullah** for everything that actually converts a DM into a partner: responding to "PILOT" DMs, the WhatsApp outreach, in-person meetings, and any PR or public naming of a signed restaurant.

### Timeline and budget
- Weeks 3–4: launch the fact-card series (2–3 posts) once Dish Drop and Sight Test have given the feed some diner-content momentum.
- Weeks 5–8: roughly one restaurant-facing post per two weeks, matching the ~1-in-5 restaurant-post ratio `social/content/plan.md` already uses.
- Weeks 9–12: if a founding restaurant signs, run the founder-story PR push and a public (consented) milestone post.
- Budget: PKR 0 for the content itself; a printed one-pager for in-person pitches costs a few hundred to low-thousand PKR (per `research/competitors.md` print-cost figures). The only real "cost" is Abdullah's time.

### KPIs and kill criterion
KPIs: DMs "PILOT" (the primary metric — this campaign's success is B2B conversion, not reach), saves/sends on the fact-card posts, and — once a founding restaurant signs — scans at that restaurant's tables as the downstream proof point. Kill criterion: zero "PILOT" DMs after six restaurant-facing posts (~12 weeks at plan.md's cadence) and no inbound interest from the founder-story PR push — pause the "Founding Tables" framing and revisit pricing/offer directly with Abdullah rather than continuing to post into silence.

### Risks and cringe check
Risk: overclaiming founding-slot scarcity if MENVA's real onboarding capacity isn't what the framing implies — only claim it to the extent it's true (Abdullah should confirm actual capacity; see Needs Abdullah). Risk: a public "what's included" question answered badly. Cringe check: no startup-pitch-deck language ("disrupting," "revolutionizing") — "founding" should read as genuinely small and real, not like a hype-driven "founding member" gimmick.

## Campaign 5 — First Reactions

**One-line idea:** A queue is free content in Lahore; MENVA has no queue, so its honest equivalent is the real, unscripted moment a diner sees their food land in AR for the first time — stitched like a highlight reel of a line moving forward.

### The insight
`research/pakistan-food-trends.md` mechanic #2 covers Tim Hortons Lahore's opening (13,000+ guests day one, Arab News) and Cinnabon's 2+ hour queues as proof that a visible line is treated as social proof and gets photographed for its own sake. The same file's mechanic #15 (Arshad Khan, the "chaiwala" — a real, unstaged street photo that outran any paid campaign and later became its own café) is the deeper point: in Pakistan's food scene, a genuinely real, unposed human moment beats a staged one, and MENVA should "make space for it" rather than manufacture it.

### The mechanic, and why it gets sends/saves/comments
Real, consenting diners are filmed with no script and no retakes — the same simple beat each time (phone lifts, dish appears in AR, face reacts) — then stitched into one reel, the way a moving queue reads as continuous proof. Real human reactions are inherently more shareable and relatable than a product shot; a good one gets sent to settle an argument or just because it's funny or surprising. It also feeds the trust loop from Dish Drop (Campaign 2): a real reaction is the strongest possible answer to "is this actually real."

### Executions

**Instagram feed post** (ready to paste, once a consenting clip exists): single best clip or short carousel of screenshots, credited or anonymized per the diner's own preference. Caption: "This is what 'see it before you order it' actually looks like. Order se pehle dekh lo — yeh raha asli reaction. See it on your table. Then order it."

**Story:** rougher, unlisted behind-the-scenes clips to reinforce that this is genuinely unstaged.

**Reel (script summary, 15–20s):** 4–5 quick clips of different diners, same beat each time (phone lifts, dish appears, reaction), soft transitions, no music loud enough to bury real reactions, ends on the sign-off ring.

**TikTok:** same cut, faster jump-cut pacing, on-screen captions of what each person actually said — their own words only, never an invented quote.

**WhatsApp:** the single best clip shared as a "see for yourself" moment.

**Offline / at the partner restaurant:** this campaign *is* the offline execution — a small table sign inviting diners to opt in ("Mind if we catch your reaction? Ask your server") makes consent clean and visible.

**PR hook:** once several strong clips exist, pitch a Lahore lifestyle outlet or a food vlogger for a cross-post or collab, letting the vlogger do their own on-camera "is it worth the hype" verdict at the restaurant.

### Partner needs
**Needs a partner for the entire campaign** — the restaurant's permission to film at the table, and each diner's real, on-record consent to appear and be published (this must be genuine, unscripted consent, never a solicited or staged "reaction," per `BRIEF.md`'s no-invented-testimonials rule). **Today**, only prep work is possible: drafting the consent-card wording and shot list so filming can start the moment consent is secured.

### Timeline and budget
- Weeks 5–6: Abdullah secures the restaurant/diner consent process, in parallel with other campaigns (non-blocking).
- Weeks 7–8: film and release the first batch — a target, not a guarantee, since it depends on real consenting moments.
- Weeks 9–12: keep filming opportunistically; release a "volume 2" if enough new clips accumulate.
- Budget: PKR 0 in production cost (filmed on a team phone during normal visits); an optional small thank-you (a comped item) for participating diners is the restaurant's decision and cost, not MENVA's.

### KPIs and kill criterion
KPIs: sends per reach (primary), comments, saves, and scans at the partner's tables in the surrounding period as a secondary signal. Kill criterion: if fewer than half of eight-plus attempted, consenting reactions produce a genuinely usable, non-awkward clip, or diners are visibly reluctant, retire the format rather than pushing people to perform for camera.

### Risks and cringe check
Consent handling is the core risk — a diner must clearly agree to appear and be published, not just be caught on camera. A reaction that reads as over-directed undercuts the entire premise. Cringe check: no "3, 2, 1, react!" instruction to camera; no dramatic music laid over real people's real, private moment.

## Campaign 6 — No Replay Needed

**One-line idea:** You can watch the match replay. Your dinner shouldn't need one — a single, pre-planned, on-brand line held ready for the morning after a major Pakistan cricket win.

### The insight
`research/pakistan-food-trends.md` mechanic #10 and case-table entry on cricket-moment brand jacking (Zomato/Blinkit's real-time posts during India–Pakistan matches — an India-side example, showing the format rather than a confirmed Pakistani case) both note that real-time posting needs an approval loop MENVA doesn't have, and that joking about the match itself risks the calm, premium brand voice. `research/genz-culture.md` confirms the same pattern from the audience side: cricket memes spike hard around outcomes but "need same-day turnaround and a light touch (never political)." Both files converge on the same conclusion already drafted in the trend-jacking research: a **pre-planned, morning-after** line is the safer, still-timely version.

### The mechanic, and why it gets sends/comments
Cricket-win mornings are one of the very few moments the whole timeline is talking about the same thing at once. A calm line that doesn't try to be a joke (avoiding the "brand tries to be funny about cricket and misses" failure mode both research files flag) rides that shared mood for reach without the risk of a punchline landing wrong.

### Executions

**Instagram feed post** (single graphic card, ready to paste, to hold and post only after a confirmed win): "Some things you find out after. Your dinner doesn't have to be one of them." Caption: "Some things you find out after. Your dinner doesn't have to be one of them. Kuch cheezein baad mein pata chalti hain. Khana pehle hi dikh jaye to behtar hai. See it on your table. Then order it." Hashtags: `#menva #lahorefood`

**Story:** reshare the same card with an open-ended "how was the match" sticker — no score predictions, nothing gambling-adjacent.

**Reel:** not recommended — a static graphic fits both the calm brand voice and a realistic same-day turnaround better than a produced video; skip video for this format.

**TikTok:** the same static card as a still-image post, or skip if the format doesn't suit a still.

**WhatsApp:** an optional same-morning single-card broadcast, only if a list exists and Abdullah wants same-day reach.

**Offline / at the partner restaurant:** none — this is a pure social moment, not a table experience.

**PR hook:** none — too small a moment to warrant outbound press; the value is purely organic same-day reach.

### Partner needs
Uses no restaurant imagery or dish-specific content beyond the brand card — **needs no partner at all.** The card can be designed and pre-approved today. **Needs Abdullah:** a same-day sign-off on tone before every single posting, tied to a real, confirmed result — no fixture date is assumed here since none was confirmed in this research.

### Timeline and budget
- Week 1: design and pre-approve the card as a ready asset (not yet posted).
- Post opportunistically whenever the next major, confirmed Pakistan win falls within the 12-week window. If none does, the asset simply carries over to the next cycle.
- Budget: PKR 0 — one in-house graphic, no paid media.

### KPIs and kill criterion
KPIs: sends per reach on the single post (this format lives or dies on same-day reach), and comments as a tone check.
