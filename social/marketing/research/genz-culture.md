# Pakistani (Lahori) Gen Z internet culture, 2025-2026 — research for MENVA marketing

Internal research notes. Not public-facing copy. Written for whoever is scripting MENVA's Instagram/TikTok content.

Per the brief: no invented stats, no invented client names or dish names, no fabricated numbers. Every claim below is either sourced with a URL, or explicitly marked "common knowledge / not independently verified in this research pass" where a search did not turn up a citable source. Where sources conflict or are thin (most Roman Urdu slang, which lives in speech and short-form video more than in indexed text), that is flagged rather than papered over.

## 1. Summary

- **TikTok is large and currently unblocked in Pakistan, but has a fragile legal history.** It has been banned and restored at least four times since 2020 over "immoral content" complaints, and remains under active government scrutiny — so a TikTok-first strategy needs an Instagram Reels mirror as insurance, not as an afterthought. Pakistan has roughly 66.9M TikTok users 18+ as of early 2025 rising to a reported 79.9M by late 2025 per TikTok's own ad-planning data relayed by DataReportal. TikTok's Pakistani ad audience skews heavily male (about 71% male / 29% female in late 2025 per DataReportal) — worth knowing since a lot of MENVA's dining-decision audience (especially "girls' hangout" and family-dining moments) may be reached more effectively on Instagram. [Digital 2026: Pakistan (DataReportal)](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-pakistan); [TikTok Pakistan Ban: Current Status, Reasons & Full Timeline (BitJoy)](https://thebitjoy.com/blogs/blog/tiktok-pakistan-ban-what-happened-why-it-keeps-happening-and-the-current-status)
- **Roman Urdu / English code-switching is the default register**, not a stylistic choice — it's how Gen Z actually talks in DMs, captions and voiceovers. Full Urdu script reads as formal/older; pure English reads as try-hard or diaspora. The sweet spot for MENVA is a short English hook + one natural Roman Urdu line, which is already the brand's stated voice rule.
- **Restaurant discovery has moved to short-form video first, then Google Maps/reviews for verification.** Industry data (not Pakistan-specific) shows TikTok driving a large share of Gen Z restaurant discovery, with nearly 40% preferring TikTok/Instagram over Search or Maps for local recommendations — Maps then gets used to confirm location, hours and check star ratings before going. [How Guests Discover New Restaurants in 2026 (Toast)](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/data/how-guests-discover-new-restaurants); [Social search is Gen Z's Google (Search Engine Land)](https://searchengineland.com/social-search-gen-z-visibility-453502)
- **Deals and discounts genuinely drive behaviour.** One source reports over 50% of Pakistani Gen Z's online purchases are discount-driven, and dedicated student-discount apps (Parchi, Dhoond, Zabardast, BOGO) exist specifically for restaurant deals — this is a live, competitive space, not a nice-to-have. [Turns out, Pakistani Gen Z is the most 'desi' of us all (Asia News Network)](https://asianews.network/turns-out-pakistani-gen-z-is-the-most-desi-of-us-all/); [Parchi — Pakistan's Student Discount App](https://www.parchipakistan.com/); [Dhoond — Student Discounts Across Pakistan](https://www.dhoond.xyz/)
- **Dining out is social infrastructure, split between family dawats and friend hangouts**, and restaurants materially design around this (separate "family halls" vs. open seating) for reasons tied to gender and class comfort as much as menu. This matters for MENVA because a diner scanning a QR code in a family hall vs. a friend-group table has a different mood and a different "who's filming" dynamic. [Sour taste: what family halls tell us (Scroll.in)](https://scroll.in/article/918812/sour-taste-what-family-halls-in-restaurants-tell-us-about-pakistans-gender-and-class-equations) — cited via search result summary; original page could not be fetched directly for full text (egress-blocked), so treat specifics inside it as secondhand until verified by reading the source in a normal browser.
- **Lahore's youth commercial geography is well-established and food-dense**: MM Alam Road / Gulberg (Xander's, Chaaye Khana, Café Aylanto, Peeru's), Johar Town's Main Boulevard (Emporium Mall, Saffron, BFC, Nando's), Bahria Town's Sector C/B strip (Johnny & Jugnu, Café Grand), and DHA. These are the obvious "rating Lahore's X" and geo-tagged content zones. [MM Alam Road Lahore guide (Raabty)](https://raabty.com/blog/mm-alam-road-lahore); [Top Cafes in Gulberg Lahore (Graana)](https://www.graana.com/blog/top-cafes-in-gulberg-lahore-you-need-to-try/); [Best Restaurants in Johar Town (Home Foodies)](https://homefoodies.pk/best-restaurants-johar-town-lahore/); [Bahria Town Lahore Restaurants List (Dogar Restaurant)](https://bahriadogarrestaurant.com/2024/11/27/bahria-town-lahore-restaurants-list-a-food-lovers-guide/)
- **Meme culture in Pakistan runs heavily on drama-serial screenshots/clips and cricket outcomes**, both extremely fast-moving and tied to the news cycle (a losing cricket match produces same-day meme floods; a viral drama character can dominate a week of feeds). [13 Viral Pakistani Drama Characters of 2025 (Brandsynario)](https://www.brandsynario.com/entertainment/celebrities/pakistani-drama/); [Memes take over social media following Pakistan's loss to India (Express Tribune)](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2530546/memes-take-over-social-media-following-pakistans-loss-to-india)
- **Halal is assumed, not a selling point, and certification-awareness is actually low.** Academic research on Pakistani Gen Z found low awareness of formal halal *certification* specifically (as opposed to the general cultural assumption that food in Pakistan is halal) — so MENVA's AR menu should carry the halal mark BRIEF.md already specifies (yes/no/unconfirmed) as a trust/clarity feature, not lean into "halal-certified" as a marketing hook, since Gen Z research shows that specific claim doesn't land the way it might elsewhere. [Generation Z's Awareness of Eating at Halal Certified Restaurants (Malaysian Journal of Agricultural Economics)](https://journals.hh-publisher.com/index.php/MJAE/article/view/328) — Malaysia-based study, use as a directional signal only, not a Pakistan-specific stat.

## 2. Slang glossary

Sourcing note: Roman Urdu Gen Z slang is overwhelmingly a spoken/short-video phenomenon, so text sources are thin and sometimes generic ("Hindi/Urdu Gen Z slang" listicles that don't distinguish Pakistan from India). Terms below are marked verified (a source discusses that exact term) vs. common knowledge (widely used in Lahori speech, matches the brief's own examples, but I could not find an independent citable source distinguishing it as specifically Pakistani Gen Z usage in this research pass).

| Term | Meaning | How MENVA could use it naturally | Cringe risk | Source / status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scene on hai | "It's happening / the plan is locked in" — also used for something exciting kicking off, or (separately) for two people starting to date | Caption on a "table's set, AR is loading" reel: "scene on hai — order karne se pehle dekh lo" | Low | Verified — [search result on "scene on hai"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394827965_Analyzing_the_use_of_Slang_by_Gen_z_in_Pakistan) |
| Kya scene hai | "What's up / what's the plan" — a genuine, versatile check-in question | Opening line of a POV reel: "Kya scene hai aaj?" then reveal the AR menu | Low | Verified — search summary citing common Gen Z usage |
| Bas kar (yaar) | "Stop it / chill" — used half-seriously to call out exaggeration or hype | Reaction line under a diner "flexing" a huge portion in AR: "bas kar yaar, itna bada?!" | Low | Verified (component "bas kar" confirmed; "…yaar" is the natural extension, common knowledge) |
| Full on | "Completely / totally," an intensifier borrowed from English but used Urdu-grammar style | "Full on real size — pinch nahi kar sakte" (playing on MENVA's true-scale AR) | Low | Common knowledge — could not confirm with an independent Pakistan-specific source; matches BRIEF.md's own listed example |
| Yaar | "Friend / dude" — near-universal filler/address term | Use as natural address in captions and voiceover, never forced ("yaar, dekho na") | Low | Verified — [Gen Z slang usage examples](https://trykaiwa.com/blog/hindi-gen-z-slang-phrases-2026) (Hindi/Urdu shared vocabulary) |
| Lit | "Exciting / amazing" | Risky in English-only form ("this is so lit") — reads try-hard from a brand account; safer folded into Roman Urdu: "bohat lit tha" as a diner's quote, not brand voice | Med | Verified — [example usage](https://trykaiwa.com/blog/hindi-gen-z-slang-phrases-2026) |
| Vibe / vibe check | Atmosphere, mood | Fine as-is, already common in Pakistani English; use for ambience, not for the food itself ("MM Alam ka vibe check" as a series title) | Low | Verified — general Gen Z usage, widely attested |
| OP | "Overpowered" (gaming-origin) — used for something excessively good/strong | High risk for MENVA: it's a gaming term first; using it for a steak ("OP cut") can land oddly outside gaming-adjacent audiences | Med-High | Not independently verified as common in Lahori food/restaurant talk specifically — treat as unconfirmed; brief lists it as an example only |
| Dawat | A hosted feast/invitation to eat — culturally loaded, warm, generous | Strong fit for MENVA's family-dining use case: "apni dawat ko full on AR mein dikhao" | Low | Verified — [Pakistani cultural norms re: dining](https://101.school/courses/learning-urdu-for-spanish-speakers/modules/4-food-and-culture/units/3-pakistani-cultural-norms-related-to-food-and-dining) |
| Flex | To show off | Good for a "diners flexing their AR-placed plate" reel format | Low | Verified — [example: "apni nayi car ka flex kar raha hai"](https://trykaiwa.com/blog/hindi-gen-z-slang-phrases-2026) |
| Mast | "Great / excellent" (older but still active across generations, not Gen-Z-coined) | Safe, warm, works for older siblings/family members in the same content | Low | Common knowledge, widely attested Pakistani/Punjabi usage, not Gen-Z-specific |
| Show the waiter | Not slang — MENVA's own product phrase | Keep exactly as brand copy specifies; do not translate or slang-ify it, it's a functional instruction | N/A | Product term, from BRIEF.md |

General rule from this research: don't stack more than one slang term per caption. The strongest, most verified pattern is **one clean English hook + one short natural Roman Urdu line**, which is also literally what BRIEF.md's voice section already asks for.

## 3. Meme / reel format landscape (context for the 20 adaptations below)

- **"Tell me you're from X without telling me"** — a flexible global format (originated Twitter 2019, went viral TikTok/Twitter late 2020) that adapts cleanly to "tell me you're from Lahore without telling me." [Tell Me Without Telling Me (Know Your Meme)](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/tell-me-without-telling-me)
- **Drama-serial meme culture** is a major, fast-cycling source of shared reference points — e.g. 2025's viral characters (Nihaal from *Dayan*, Sameer from *Parwarish*) show how a single drama moment can dominate feeds for weeks. Using drama-style reaction shots/audio (not copyrighted clips themselves) as a format, not specific IP, is the safe lane. [13 Viral Pakistani Drama Characters of 2025 (Brandsynario)](https://www.brandsynario.com/entertainment/celebrities/pakistani-drama/)
- **Cricket memes** spike hard around match outcomes (wins and, especially, losses against India) — a real-time opportunity format if MENVA ever wants to ride a trending moment, but needs same-day turnaround and a light touch (never political). [Memes take over social media following Pakistan's loss to India (Express Tribune)](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2530546/memes-take-over-social-media-following-pakistans-loss-to-india)
- **"Things that just make sense"** — originally a home-tour format (viral April 2020), now genre-agnostic ("things that just make sense in my [X]"). Adapts well to "things that just make sense on a Lahore dinner table." [Things That Just Make Sense (Know Your Meme)](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/things-that-just-make-sense)
- **GRWM (get ready with me)** has fused with ASMR and mukbang formats generally, and Pakistani creators are already making ASMR/mukbang food content with desi dishes. [What is GRWM? (Qustodio)](https://www.qustodio.com/en/blog/what-is-grwm-and-what-does-it-mean/); [Pakistani Food ASMR Mukbangs](https://www.tiktok.com/@thefoodvilla_/video/7276577913888050437)
- **Rating-format content** ("rate this a 10/10") is an established Instagram hook style and maps naturally onto rating dishes, table views, or AR moments. [Instagram Trends: September 2026 (Newengen)](https://newengen.com/insights/instagram-trends/)
- Could not independently verify Pakistan-specific data on "day in my life" or "street food vs fine dining" as named micro-trends beyond general confirmation that day-in-the-life university content is common at LUMS/UCP/Punjab University — treated as common knowledge for format purposes, not cited as a proven Pakistan trend.

## 5. Do and don't — voice rules

**Do**
- Lead with one clean English hook line, then one natural Roman Urdu line — matches how Lahori Gen Z actually code-switches in captions and speech.
- Use "yaar," "scene on hai," "bas kar," "full on," and "dawat" sparingly and only where they'd naturally fall in a sentence a real person would say.
- Reference real, specific Lahore geography (MM Alam Road, Gulberg, Johar Town, DHA, Bahria) when it's true to the content — specificity reads as authentic, generic "foodie content" doesn't.
- Keep captions short. Gen Z scroll-speed means the hook has to land in the first three words on screen.
- Write dish references only as "the steak board," "the steak sandwich," or generically ("the dish," "this plate") — never invent or guess at the two unconfirmed dish names.
- Keep sentence case, no emoji, calm-warm-playful tone — this is already the brand rule and it also happens to fit how the more design-conscious slice of Lahori Gen Z content (Xander's/Chaaye Khana-adjacent, MM Alam aesthetic) already talks.

**Don't**
- Don't stack three or four slang terms in one caption — it reads like a brand trying too hard, which is the fastest way to get mocked in the comments.
- Don't use "OP," "lit," or gaming/global-TikTok slang as the primary hook — these are unverified for Lahori food-content usage and risk feeling imported rather than local.
- Don't touch cricket-loss or political meme moments unless there's a same-day, very light, non-partisan angle — moving fast on a stale cricket meme is worse than not doing one.
- Don't use any drama-serial clip, music or character likeness that isn't licensed — the drama-meme *format* (reaction style, pacing, on-screen text) is fair game; the actual footage is not MENVA's to use.
- Don't claim "halal-certified" as a marketing hook — research suggests Gen Z awareness of formal certification is low and it can read as an unverifiable claim; use the product's built-in halal mark (yes/no/unconfirmed) instead, exactly as BRIEF.md specifies.
- Don't name the pilot restaurant, the two unconfirmed dish names, or any PKR price for the diner-facing content — pricing shown should only ever be the restaurant's own menu prices, never MENVA's PKR 25,000 pilot fee, which is a B2B fact, not diner-facing content.
- Don't fabricate a "reaction" from a real influencer or diner without their actual, on-record permission — reposting or restaging someone else's words as if MENVA verified them would violate the brief's no-invented-testimonials rule.
