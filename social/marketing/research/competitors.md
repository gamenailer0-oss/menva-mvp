## 3. Evidence on whether 3D/AR menus or dish photos raise orders, AOV or confidence

Ranked from strongest to weakest evidence. **Be honest with this table in sales conversations — do not upgrade a lab study into a guarantee.**

| Source | Type | Sample / setting | What it actually found | Caveats / how strong is this, really |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fritz, Hadi & Stephen, "From tablet to table: How augmented reality influences food desirability," *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science*, vol. 51, 2023, pp. 503–529** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-022-00919-x), [Oxford Research Archive](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:db0a5980-51dd-4281-b785-ba8591696f60), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36589144/)) | **Peer-reviewed field experiment** (plus lab studies) in a top marketing journal. One of the authors (Andrew Stephen) is at Oxford's Saïd Business School, which is why press coverage refers to it as an "Oxford University" study; it also appears to be the study behind the QReal-linked press coverage of the same numbers. | **Field Study 1: 101 real diners** at a real restaurant in "a large international city," two consecutive weekday dinner shifts. Diners were shown either an AR version or a plain digital version of the **dessert menu only**, then their actual orders and spend were recorded (participants got a £5 bill discount for taking part). | Diners shown the AR dessert menu were **significantly more likely to order a dessert: 41.2% vs 18%** in the control group, and spent more on dessert when they did: **$2.93 vs $1.38 average — a 112% increase** ([The Spoon summary of the paper](https://thespoon.tech/as-menus-move-to-mobile-phones-research-shows-ar-could-drive-more-sales/)). Mechanism proposed: AR increases "mental simulation" of eating the food, which drives desire and purchase likelihood. | **This is the strongest evidence found** — real money, real diners, a real restaurant, peer-reviewed. But: single restaurant, single city, dessert category only (not steaks or mains), n=101 is modest, and it tested *a* AR menu, not MENVA's specific product. Do not claim "112% AOV lift" for MENVA — that number is specific to dessert add-on orders in someone else's study, not a MENVA result. |
| **Seo et al., Washington State University, published in a hospitality/marketing journal, covered Feb 2026** ([WSU Insider](https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2026/02/18/augmented-reality-menus-may-help-restaurants-attract-customers/), [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1116819), [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0278431925002634)) | **Academic, but lab/survey-based**, not a field purchase study. | Two experiments; Study 1 showed participants one of three simulated menu formats (printed, QR, AR) for a burger with farm-to-table sourcing info; Study 2 compared brand contexts (Panera vs McDonald's). | Participants reported **higher intention to visit the restaurant**, more willingness to share the menu with others, and felt more "immersed" and better informed about sourcing, after viewing the AR format — compared with printed/QR. | This measures **stated intention and feeling**, not actual orders or spend — a real but weaker form of evidence than the field study above. Useful for "AR menus feel more informative and shareable," not for "AR menus increase sales" claims. |
| **Kabaq/QReal's own claim: "800+ restaurants... 20–26% AOV uplift... 90 days"** | Vendor marketing stat, quoted in a third-party SEO/blog roundup, not the primary source itself. | Unknown — no published methodology found. | AOV uplift of 20–26%, "22% reduction in order errors." | **Weakest tier: an unverified vendor claim**, repeated by secondary blogs, with no visible underlying data, sample, or independent audit. Treat as marketing copy, not evidence, in any pitch. |
| **DoorDash/Panera Snapchat AR Lens** | Single ad-campaign case study. | 4.5M impressions, ~3% of swipers made a purchase, on $50K spend. | Framed by secondary sources as proof AR "delivers excellent ROI." | This measures an **ad campaign's conversion rate**, not a dish-visualization effect on in-restaurant ordering — a different mechanism (social AR advertising vs. a menu tool) than MENVA. Do not cite specific figures from it without reading the actual abstract/paper. |

**Honest summary for sales use:** the one solid, real-money, peer-reviewed data point is the Oxford/JAMS dessert study (41.2% vs 18% order rate, 112% dessert spend increase) — and even that is one restaurant, one city, one category (dessert), n=101. Every other "AR increases sales" number circulating online either restates that same study without attribution, is a vendor's unaudited self-report (Kabaq/QReal's 20–26%), or measures stated intention/ad-campaign clicks rather than actual restaurant orders. **No study of any kind was found for Pakistan, for Lahore, for steaks specifically, or for MENVA's own product.** Any pitch line should say "an Oxford field study found AR menus roughly doubled dessert spend in one UK restaurant" — not "AR menus double your revenue."

## 5. Positioning map

Axis: **effort/cost to the diner to understand a dish** (low → high) vs. **fidelity to what actually arrives at the table** (low → high).

| Option | Diner effort | Fidelity to real dish | Real-time / per-table | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper menu, text only | Low (read) | None (imagination only) | No | Universal baseline; zero cost after printing. Lahore printing runs roughly PKR 300–1,000/card depending on size/finish, or ~PKR 55/unit for a basic single-page letter-size menu (see Section 6). |
| Photo menu (printed or laminated, restaurant's own photos) | Low (glance) | Low-medium — a photo is not the plate that arrives, often styled/lit differently, ages badly | No | Common upgrade; cost is a one-off photoshoot + print (see Section 6). |
| QR → PDF/image menu (MenuQR-style, POS-bundled) | Low (scan, scroll) | Same as a photo menu — it's just the same static photos on a phone | No | This is Lahore's real, most common "digital menu" today; often free or bundled with a POS subscription. |
| Delivery-app photos (foodpanda etc.) | Low (browse app) | Low-medium, but foodpanda enforces a real photo-quality bar (4000×2925px, top/front view, no branding) — often better than what a restaurant's own printed menu has | No | This is arguably the toughest incumbent MENVA has to beat on visual quality, and diners already trust it for delivery decisions. |
| Google Business Profile photos | Low (search) | Variable — crowd-sourced and owner photos mixed, quality inconsistent | No | Free, ubiquitous, zero effort for the restaurant, but no control over quality. |
| Overseas AR menu vendors (Kabaq/QReal, ARmenu, AR Code, etc.) | Medium (scan, tap, AR permission) | High — genuine 3D scans / real dish models | Partially — dish opens on demand, no live per-table state | Not present in Pakistan. Priced per dish (ARmenu from £49) or as an unclear SaaS (Kabaq/QReal). Some rely on GenAI-rendered dishes rather than real scans — a trust gap MENVA's "real scans only" rule avoids. |
| **MENVA** | Medium (scan QR, tap dish, allow AR) | **Highest** — real 3D scan of the actual plate, true-size AR placement on the diner's own table, photo-first fallback for slow wifi | Yes — table-aware, waiter-facing "show the waiter" list, pilot analytics per scan/open/AR-launch | No online ordering (by design — waiter stays in charge); no app; built for Lahore restaurant wifi conditions specifically. |

### MENVA's defensible angles
- **Real scans, not renders, not stock, not AI.** Every competitor search turned up vendors that will happily generate or stylize dish imagery (some explicitly market "AR GenAI" for speed); MENVA's real-scan-only rule is a genuine differentiator against a market that increasingly reaches for AI shortcuts. Diners can be told, honestly, "this is the actual dish."
- **True-size AR placement, not resizable.** Locking AR scale (no pinch-resize) is unusual in this review — most competitor marketing copy talks about "seeing the dish in 3D," not "seeing the dish at true size, non-adjustable." This maps directly to a real, sourced problem: portion-size uncertainty is exactly what the Oxford field study's mechanism (desire driven by mental simulation of eating the real thing) targets.
- **Built for the actual network conditions of a Lahore restaurant** (photo-first, 3D follows) — no competitor material reviewed mentions engineering specifically for weak restaurant wifi; most assume good connectivity.
- **No app, in-browser AR, waiter stays in charge.** This matches the *mechanics* of the best-positioned overseas comparable (ARmenu: QR → browser AR, no app), but none of the reviewed vendors operate in Pakistan, so MENVA is not fighting another AR company locally — it's introducing the category.
- **Analytics diners' restaurants don't currently get** from a paper or QR-PDF menu: scans, dish opens, AR launches, "show the waiter" events. POS-bundled QR tools (CORN, Foodnerd) offer ordering/inventory analytics, not this kind of interest/engagement signal.

### Honest weak spots
- **No independent evidence for MENVA specifically, anywhere.** The one solid academic result (112% dessert spend, 41.2% vs 18% order rate) is from an unrelated UK study, one restaurant, one category. It is evidence AR *can* work, not evidence MENVA *will* work in Lahore. Selling it as anything more than "this is what's been found elsewhere" would break the brief's "never invent stats" rule.
- **Only 4 real dish scans exist right now**, two of them unconfirmed by name — the current pilot inventory is thin relative to a full menu.
- **foodpanda's photo bar is already decent** and free to the restaurant (comes with the delivery listing) — "a photo isn't enough" is a harder sell where diners already see good photos daily.
- **PKR 25,000 pilot price has no comparable local AR benchmark** — there is no Pakistani AR-menu competitor to price against, so the number will be judged against photography/printing/influencer costs instead (see Section 6), not against a "competitor" price.
- **Real 3D scanning is the operational bottleneck**, not the AR delivery layer — every credible competitor (Kabaq/QReal, ARmenu, AR Code) treats scanning as the slow, expensive, non-scalable part of the business; MENVA will face the same constraint as it tries to grow past 4 dishes and past [pilot restaurant].
- **No AR-native ordering path** (by design) means MENVA cannot claim the direct online-ordering AOV lift language some competitors imply, since MENVA has no checkout at all — "show the waiter" is a different mechanism than "add to cart," and any AOV claim needs to be reasoned through that difference, not borrowed from a checkout-based study.
