# How Pakistani food and drink brands went viral (2022–2026) — and what MENVA can take from it

Internal research doc. Written for the MENVA marketing strategy. All claims below are sourced; anything without a source URL is marked **unverified** and should not be repeated as fact in public copy. Nothing here is public-facing copy — it is raw material for the strategy file.

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. The single biggest lever in this whole research set is **scarcity plus a reveal**: Crumbl-style cookie shops built a national trend on "you only get this flavour for six days," and the reveal (box opening, taste, reaction) is the content, not the ad.
2. **Queues are free content**, and Pakistani Twitter/X turns them into a national conversation whether the brand wants that or not (Tim Hortons, Cinnabon) — that cuts both ways: reach, but also class-and-inflation backlash.
3. **Meme banter between brands** (Crumble vs Blinkit, Tapal vs Lipton) consistently outperforms polished ads for reach, because it looks like it wasn't planned.
4. **A funny, low-budget ad built around one recurring bit or actor** (Johnny & Jugnu's "talcum powder guy") can carry a brand's identity for years — cheaper and stickier than a campaign-by-campaign approach.
5. **Global dessert trends (Dubai/kunafa chocolate, tres leches/three milk cake) land in Pakistan within weeks**, via home-based bakers and TikTok/Instagram ASMR-style videos, before any big chain reacts — small, fast movers win the trend window.
6. **Roman Urdu, not English, is what makes a line stick** — foodpanda's "Sambhal Lega" and Tapal's "Danedaar" lines work because they read like something a person would actually say, not a slogan.
7. **Food vloggers and "is it worth the hype" review culture is the trust layer** in Pakistan's food scene — a brand's own posts get discounted, but a known food vlogger's verdict travels.
8. **Founder-and-team-on-camera content works for smaller/independent brands** (Crumble's founders, students running a cookie business) — it reads as honest in a way agency-made ads don't.
9. **Being "the Instagrammable spot"** (Howdy's rooftop, cowboy-themed interiors, food-street aesthetics) is itself a marketing channel — people photograph the place, not just the food.
10. **Platform risk is real**: Crumble Pakistan's Instagram (203k followers, the account driving most of its hype) was taken down by copyright reports in July 2025 — a brand that lives entirely on one platform's account can lose it overnight.

MENVA does not sell food, so none of this can be copied literally. The throughline that *does* transfer: Pakistani food hype is built on **doubt resolved in public** — "is it really that big," "is it really worth the price," "is this even real." MENVA's actual product — a real 3D scan you place at true size on your own table, before you order — is a direct, honest answer to that exact doubt. That is the angle worth building the whole content strategy around.

## 3. The 12–15 repeatable hype mechanics — and how MENVA adapts each one

MENVA sells a QR-to-AR dish viewer, not food. It cannot show hype for a dish it hasn't scanned, cannot invent stats or dishes, cannot use AI food imagery, cannot name the pilot restaurant without Abdullah's OK, and can only truthfully claim what the four real scans show (the steak board, the steak sandwich, "the green plate," and "the trio" — the last two names are unconfirmed and must never be invented). Every idea below is written inside those limits. Where an idea needs the pilot restaurant's dish, food, staff or premises on camera, or needs anything published, it is flagged for Abdullah.

### 1. Scarcity and rotating reveal
**How it works:** Only one flavour/dish is available for a short window, so showing up (or watching the reveal video) matters this week specifically.
**Pakistani example:** Crumble's weekly rotating cookie flavours, six-day availability window. [Aurora](https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1145412/the-way-the-reels-crumble)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA doesn't control the menu, but it can run a **"dish of the week" AR spotlight** — one of the four real scans gets featured across a week, with a short making-of angle (how the scan was captured, what "true size" means for that specific dish). It's honest scarcity: there are only four scans that exist, so featuring them one at a time, in rotation, is simply accurate, not manufactured hype.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "This week on the pass: the steak sandwich, at true size."
- Format: 12–15 second vertical reel — QR scan → phone lifts → AR model settles on a real table, static beat, cut to the size against a hand or a fork for scale.
- Caption (English): "No filter, no guessing. Scan it, see it at true size, then order it."
- Roman Urdu line: "Andaza nahi, seedha dekh lo — asal size mein, order se pehle."

### 2. Queue / opening-day hype as content
**How it works:** A visible queue is treated as social proof; people photograph the line itself, not just the product.
**Pakistani example:** Tim Hortons Lahore opening (13,000+ guests day one). [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2254026/lifestyle)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA has no queue of its own. The honest equivalent is a **"first reactions" reel** — several diners' actual, unscripted reactions the moment the AR dish lands on their table, stitched together like a highlight reel of a queue moving forward. This needs the pilot restaurant and real diners on camera.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "What happens the first time someone sees their food before it arrives."
- Format: 4–5 quick clips of different tables, same beat each time — phone up, dish appears, person's face reacts — stitched with a soft transition, no music that overpowers the reactions.
- Caption (English): "This is what 'see it before you order it' actually looks like."
- Roman Urdu line: "Order se pehle dekh lo — yeh raha asli reaction."
- **Needs Abdullah:** requires the pilot restaurant's permission, real diners' consent to be filmed, and Abdullah's sign-off to publish.

### 3. Unboxing / first-look reveal
**How it works:** The moment of opening a box (Crumbl-style pink box, PR box) is the content — anticipation, then payoff.
**Pakistani example:** Crumble's pink-box aesthetic and unboxing culture on TikTok. [TikTok discovery](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/crumble-cookies-pakistan)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA's "box" is the phone screen. The equivalent reveal is **the loading moment itself** — "The Pass" loader (per the build spec) going from blurred poster photo to sharp focus to full 3D, ending in AR placement. That sequence, shown uncut, is a legitimate unboxing.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "Watch the dish come into focus."
- Format: screen-recording style reel — blurred photo sharpening, ring tracing progress, then the crossfade to 3D, then AR placement on a table, no cuts, real load time.
- Caption (English): "No trick. This is the app loading, start to finish."
- Roman Urdu line: "Yeh koi trick nahi — bas app load ho rahi hai, shuru se aakhir tak."

### 4. Taste-test and reaction videos → "sight-test" videos
**How it works:** Someone tries the product on camera and reacts in real time; the reaction is the proof.
**Pakistani example:** Widespread food-vlogger reaction/review culture (Hamza Bhatti, Saqib Mobeen, Adeel Siddiqui and others). [LocalWriter roundup](https://localwriter.pk/top-food-vloggers-in-pakistan/)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA can't offer taste, but it can offer the equivalent **sight-test**: a diner (or a food creator, with permission) seeing the AR-placed dish next to the real dish once it arrives, comparing scale and presentation honestly.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "We put the AR version next to the real plate. Here's what happened."
- Format: split-screen — left: AR dish on the table before ordering; right: the real dish arriving; no narration needed, just the two side by side.
- Caption (English): "Same size. Same dish. We just let you see it first."
- Roman Urdu line: "Size wohi, dish wohi — bas order se pehle dikha diya."
- **Needs Abdullah:** restaurant + diner/creator permission to film and publish.

### 5. "Is it worth the hype?" verdict format
**How it works:** A creator or the brand itself explicitly answers the "is this actually good / worth the price / worth the wait" question the audience is already asking.
**Pakistani example:** Pakistan's food-vlogger review culture generally frames content this way (verdict-driven reviews). [Modash Lahore food influencer list](https://www.modash.io/find-influencers/pakistan/lahore/food)
**MENVA adaptation:** Reframe the question from "is the food worth it" to **"is the AR real or is it a trick?"** — since that is the actual skepticism MENVA will face (people assume it's CGI or AI). Answer it directly and repeatedly, on camera, as a format.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "Is this a real scan or CGI? We'll show you exactly how it's made."
- Format: short explainer reel — a phone or camera scanning the actual plate, the raw scan data appearing, then the finished AR model — no AI tools anywhere in the sequence, shown on screen.
- Caption (English): "Real dish, real scan. No AI, ever."
- Roman Urdu line: "Asli dish, asli scan — AI ka koi kaam nahi."
- **Needs Abdullah:** requires access to film the scanning process, which touches the pilot restaurant's kitchen/dish.

### 6. Price reveal / value framing
**How it works:** Naming a number — a price, a wait time, a portion size — on camera, as content.
**Pakistani example:** Deal-price TikTok reels are a standard format for chains like Ranchers and Burger Lab (e.g. "149 deal"). [TikTok — Ranchers deal](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/ranchers-islamabad-f-11-burger-deal-149)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA cannot invent or disclose restaurant menu prices — those belong to the restaurant. What it *can* honestly show is the **portion-size reveal**: placing the AR dish against a real object (a hand, a standard plate, a ruler) so the size claim is checkable on the spot, publicly, every time.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "True size means true size. Watch."
- Format: AR dish placed next to a tape measure or a standard dinner plate, static shot, no zoom tricks.
- Caption (English): "It can't be resized in AR. What you see is what's coming."
- Roman Urdu line: "AR mein size chhota-bara nahi hota — jo dikh raha hai wohi aayega."

### 7. Founder / builder on camera
**How it works:** The person behind the brand explains it directly, unscripted — reads as more honest than an ad.
**Pakistani example:** Crumble's founders (GIKI students) speaking directly to camera about the business. [The Markhor Times](https://themarkhortimes.com/opinion/crumble-pakistan-the-student-run-cookie-business/)
**MENVA adaptation:** Abdullah explaining, plainly, why MENVA exists — the "see it before you order it" idea, why no AI images, why the waiter stays in charge. Calm, not salesy, matching the brand voice.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "Why I built a menu you can't lie to."
- Format: single static shot, Abdullah talking for 30–40 seconds, one real dish visible on a table beside him.
- Caption (English): "The idea behind MENVA, in plain words."
- Roman Urdu line: "MENVA kyun banaya — seedhi baat, meri zubaani."
- **Needs Abdullah:** this entire idea requires Abdullah to appear on camera and approve publishing.

### 8. A recurring, restrained visual signature (not a cartoon mascot)
**How it works:** A repeated character or motif gives chaotic content a shape people recognise instantly.
**Pakistani example:** Crumble's fictional cat "Meowbaksh" as a running bit across posts. [Slurry Digital](https://slurrydigital.com/2024/11/04/how-crumble-pakistan-is-taking-over-instagram-with-meme-marketing/)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA's brand voice is explicitly "never cartoonish," so a mascot character is the wrong tool. The adaptation is a **restrained recurring device** instead — the same chili-red progress ring and the same closing line ("See it on your table. Then order it.") at the end of every piece of content, so the format itself becomes recognisable without a character.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: (varies per post)
- Format rule: every video ends on the same 1-second beat — the chili-red ring closing to full, cream background, wordmark.
- Caption sign-off (English, used consistently): "See it on your table. Then order it."
- Roman Urdu sign-off (used consistently): "Apni table pe dekho. Phir order karo."

### 9. Meme replies and brand banter
**How it works:** Two brands trade public jabs; the audience picks a side and shares it.
**Pakistani example:** Crumble vs Blinkit comment-section exchange. [Brandsynario](https://www.brandsynario.com/blinkit-vs-crumble-pakistan-the-hilarious-cross-border-banter/)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA has no direct competitor brand to spar with in Pakistan yet, and picking a fight as an unproven startup can look try-hard. The safer, still-playful version is **replying to skepticism in comments with the same calm confidence**, publicly, so the reply itself becomes shareable proof rather than a brand feud.
**Concrete idea:**
- Trigger: a comment like "yeh sirf CGI hai" ("this is just CGI") under a MENVA reel.
- Reply (English): "Fair question — it's a real 3D scan of the actual dish, not a render. Here's the scan itself: [link]."
- Reply (Roman Urdu): "Sahi sawal — yeh asli dish ka real scan hai, CGI nahi. Scan khud dekh lo: [link]."
- **Needs Abdullah:** none to draft replies, but public brand-voice replies should follow the same "Needs Abdullah" publishing rule as any other public post if a social manager other than Abdullah is posting under the brand account.

### 10. Cricket-moment / real-time cultural jacking
**How it works:** Brands post within minutes of a live cricket moment to ride the trending conversation.
**Pakistani example:** Zomato/Blinkit real-time posts during India–Pakistan matches. [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/ind-vs-pak-formers-victory-turns-internet-into-a-meme-fest-by-brands-social-accounts-57499.htm)
**MENVA adaptation:** Real-time posting needs a fast approval loop MENVA doesn't have yet, and joking about cricket outcomes risks looking off-brand for a calm, premium voice. Lower-risk version: a **pre-planned line for the next major Pakistan cricket fixture**, tying "seeing it before it happens" to the match, prepared in advance and posted once the result is known — not truly real-time, but timely.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "You can watch the replay. Or you can see your dinner before it lands — no replay needed."
- Format: single graphic/text card in brand style (no AI food imagery, no player likenesses), posted the morning after a major win.
- Caption (English): "Some things you find out after. Your dinner doesn't have to be one of them."
- Roman Urdu line: "Kuch cheezein baad mein pata chalti hain. Khana pehle hi dikh jaye to behtar hai."
- **Needs Abdullah:** sign-off before posting anything tied to a live national event, to check tone against the moment.

### 11. Ramadan / Eid emotional seasonal campaigns
**How it works:** A brand ties itself to a shared emotional beat of the season (homesickness, generosity, gathering) rather than a hard sell.
**Pakistani example:** Shan Foods' Ramadan TVC ("Ami ki bohot yaad arahi hai bhai"); Cheezious's Independence Day "Azadi, Cheez Hai Aesi." [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/08/12/how-cheezious-redefines-freedom-with-azadi-cheez-hai-aesi/)
**MENVA adaptation:** During Ramadan/Eid, restaurants using MENVA get very high covers; the natural angle is **"no guessing what you're ordering when you're fasting and hungry"** — a genuinely useful framing for iftar ordering, not just a seasonal ad.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "Iftar mein guess nahi karna padta — dekh kar order karo."
- Format: short reel, AR dish placed on an iftar table setting (real dish, real restaurant, with permission), single line of copy.
- Caption (English): "At iftar, every minute counts. See the dish, then order — no back-and-forth with the waiter."
- Roman Urdu line: "Iftar pe waqt kam hota hai — dish dekho, phir order karo."
- **Needs Abdullah:** pilot restaurant permission for an iftar-setting shoot; timing needs to align with Ramadan dates.

### 12. Trend-jacking a global viral food
**How it works:** A trend that's already viral elsewhere (Dubai chocolate, tres leches) gets localised fast by small, nimble sellers.
**Pakistani example:** Kunafa/Dubai chocolate bars recreated by Pakistani home bakers within the same viral cycle as the UAE original. [Muffin Club](https://muffinclub.com.pk/products/kunafa-chocolate)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA can't sell a trending dessert, but it can **comment on the trend from its own angle** — using the "is it really that big / is it worth the hype" energy of a viral food moment to talk about why seeing true size matters, without depicting a dish MENVA hasn't scanned (no AI imagery, ever).
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "Every viral dessert this year had the same question under it: 'is it actually that big?'"
- Format: text-forward reel — screenshots of real public comments asking about size (credited/blurred appropriately), ending on MENVA's own AR placement of one of its real scans.
- Caption (English): "We built the answer to that question. See it at true size, before you order."
- Roman Urdu line: "Yehi sawal har viral dessert ke neeche hota hai — hum ne iska jawab bana diya."

### 13. "Instagrammable spot" as its own channel
**How it works:** A physical space designed to be photographed generates content without the brand lifting a finger.
**Pakistani example:** Howdy Rooftop's cowboy-themed, photogenic interior. [Brandsynario](https://www.brandsynario.com/7-instagram-worthy-restaurants-in-lahore/)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA doesn't own a physical space, but it can make **the AR moment itself the "photographable" thing** — encouraging diners to film their own phone screen with the dish floating on their real table, since that visual (a life-size dish appearing to float above a real tablecloth) is inherently novel and shareable.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "Get the shot before you get the bill."
- Format: a simple in-restaurant table tent or QR card: "Scan. See it. Film it." with a small MENVA mark — encourages diners to record their own AR placement moment.
- Caption for diners to use (English, suggested, not mandatory): "My dinner, before it was my dinner."
- Roman Urdu suggested line: "Khana aane se pehle hi dekh liya."
- **Needs Abdullah:** approval for any physical table-tent design and print cost at the pilot restaurant.

### 14. Local slang and Roman Urdu wordplay
**How it works:** A line that sounds like something a person would actually say out loud beats a translated slogan every time.
**Pakistani example:** foodpanda's "Sambhal Lega," Tapal's "Danedaar" line. [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/paywall/article/warc-awards-effectiveness/foodpanda-pakistan-sambhal-lega--getting-better-everyday/en-gb/158766)
**MENVA adaptation:** Every public post pairs an English line with a natural Lahori Roman Urdu line (already required by the brief) — the discipline here is making sure the Roman Urdu line is never a literal translation, but something a Lahori would actually text a friend.
**Concrete idea:**
- Hook line: "Dhoka nahi, dikhawa hai — asli."
- Format: usable as a general brand tagline test across a few posts to see which Roman Urdu phrasing gets the most saves/shares.
- Caption (English): "No surprises. What you see is what arrives."
- Roman Urdu line: "Koi dhoka nahi — jo dikha wohi aayega."

### 15. The everyday-hero moment (organic, unplanned virality)
**How it works:** Sometimes the biggest moment isn't a campaign at all — a real, human, unstaged moment gets shared because it's genuinely interesting.
**Pakistani example:** Arshad Khan, the "chaiwala," went viral in 2016 purely from a street photo, with no marketing spend, and later built his own café off that fame. [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/pakistan/remember-pakistans-chaiwala-who-went-viral-in-2016-he-just-launched-his-own-caf-1.1601917609726)
**MENVA adaptation:** MENVA can't manufacture this, but it can **make space for it** — by filming real, unscripted first-time reactions (see mechanic #2) and being ready to amplify a genuinely surprising one if it happens, rather than only ever posting pre-approved content.
**Concrete idea:**
- Practice, not a single post: keep every diner-reaction clip raw and unedited-looking (no heavy filters, no trend audio layered over real reactions) so that if one moment does break out organically, it reads as real, not staged.
- **Needs Abdullah:** a standing decision on how fast the team can approve and repost a spontaneous, high-potential clip without losing the "it looked real" quality by over-editing it.

## 5. The big risks

- **Looking cringe.** Several of the mechanics above (mascot chaos, meme banter, cricket-jacking) work because they match a Gen-Z, chaotic, fast-food voice. MENVA's brand voice is explicitly calm, warm, a little playful, "never cartoonish" — copying Crumble's chaos directly would clash with the brand and read as try-hard. Every idea above has been adapted down to match MENVA's actual register; that discipline needs to hold even under pressure to "go viral."
- **Hype without product.** Tim Hortons and Cinnabon had queues because there was a real, immediate thing to queue for. MENVA's hype has to be earned by the AR experience actually working well in the restaurant, every time — content that oversells the experience before it's reliably fast and glitch-free risks a public "this didn't work" backlash, which is much more damaging for a small local startup than for an international chain.
- **Naming brands or restaurants without permission.** Several case-table examples here (Cheezious, Crumble, Tim Hortons) are public because the brands themselves published the content. MENVA cannot name the pilot restaurant, cannot show its dishes, and cannot use any restaurant's name, logo or premises in public content without that restaurant's explicit sign-off — this applies to every trend-jacking idea above marked "needs restaurant permission."
- **Platform dependency.** Crumble Pakistan's Instagram — the account carrying most of its hype — was taken down in July 2025 over copyright complaints and had to be rebuilt. MENVA should not treat any single platform account as a permanent asset; owned channels (the website, an email/WhatsApp list if one exists) matter as backup.
- **Cricket and national-moment jacking can misfire.** The Tim Hortons queue backlash shows how fast a "look how popular we are" moment can flip into a class/inequality criticism in Pakistan. Cricket-moment content in particular needs a tone check before posting, not just speed.
- **Unverifiable claims creeping into public copy.** Some sources in this research (the Tapal/Lipton chai-wars taglines, the Nashville hot chicken detail, the Shan Foods Ramadan campaign specifics, the "expectation vs reality" meme as a Pakistan-specific phenomenon) could not be independently confirmed against a primary source in this pass and are marked unverified above. None of it should be repeated as fact in MENVA's own public content without a stronger source.
